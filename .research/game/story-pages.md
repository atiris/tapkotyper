# Story pages and fact cards

## What
- A calm "reading corner" on the map: the child types a short page of Tapko's story book
  (3-6 sentences) and the picture on the page comes to life as the text is typed (the
  dragon flies once its sentence is done, the egg cracks at the last word).
- Fact cards: short, true facts about the animals, space and dragons-in-myths of each
  world ("A bee visits about 50 flowers on one trip."), one per page, collected in a small
  album.
- Pages open after the sentence track is unlocked and use only known keys where possible;
  a page waits for the child, nothing moves or runs out.

## Why
- A story mode where the narrative appears as you type and pauses until you type the correct
  letters is described as giving the feeling of writing a short story and bringing it to life.
- Repetitive drills are the main complaint about typing sites ("repetitive lesson structure")
  and low replay value is a top complaint about typing games.
- Typing woven into a story is loved, as a more relaxed typing game than its peers.
- Sentences that are not always kid friendly are criticised - hand-written child-safe text
  is a plus.

## Fit
- Extends the existing story without new pressure; good use of the sentence track.
- Risk: writing and translating text in two languages; start with one page per level.
- Collecting fact cards is progress-based, never random.

## Priority
P2, effort M. Depends on [sentences-and-punctuation](../learning/sentences-and-punctuation.md).
