# Tapko asks, the child answers (own writing)

## What
- Sometimes (for example at the end of a session) Tapko asks a simple question: "What is
  your favourite animal?", "What would you pack for space?", "Give a name to the baby
  dragon."
- The child types a free answer. There is no right or wrong; mistakes are not marked, only
  the finger guide stays available.
- Answers are kept in "Tapko's diary" inside the profile; some answers are used in the game
  (the dragon's name shows on the map, Tapko repeats the favourite animal later).
- Always optional ("Not now" button); the parent can switch it off.

## Why
- Real-world typing is composing, not copying. Research summaries note the shift from
  "transcription typing" to "generative typing", which "helps student writing develop
  faster" (Zeitz, via https://blog.tcea.org/tag/tutorial/).
- A Vanderbilt study saw a fast typist drop "from 78 to 45 words per minute" when typing
  their own text instead of copying
  (https://mentalfloss.com/article/87672/forget-mavis-beacon-self-taught-typists-are-nearly-fast-touch-typists),
  so composing is its own skill that needs practice.
- A small study with special-needs pupils says keyboarding gives struggling writers "an
  alternative to the traditional paper and pencil method" (https://vtext.valdosta.edu/xmlui/handle/10428/142).
- Naming and personal choices make the companion "the child's own", which the app already
  values (custom messages, wardrobe).

## Fit
- Strong fit: personal, calm, private (diary stays local and in the export).
- Risk: free text may contain anything; it is never shown to others and only used
  locally. Keep questions short for 8-year-olds.

## Priority
P2, effort S-M.
