# Hold keys and reach: ideas for steady fingers

Date: 2026-11-04. A brainstorm: main ideas, not a design yet.

## 1. One hand holds, the other types (P1)
- **Idea:** one hand holds its home key (F or J) down for the whole step, the other hand types words of
  its own keys.
- **Why:** the resting finger cannot drift away, so home becomes a place the hand always returns to. The
  typing hand gets no help from the other one.
- **When:** early, once the child knows at least four keys of each hand.
- **Calm rule:** when the held key is let go, the scene waits ("Put your finger back on J") and goes on
  when it is pressed again. Nothing is lost.
- **Later:** the same on the number row (1-5 while J is held, 6-0 while F is held).

## 2. Several fingers hold, one finger taps (P1)
- **Idea:** the other fingers of a hand stay pressed on their home keys, and one finger reaches and
  taps. For example hold `a s d` and tap `f r t`, or hold `j l` and tap `ä`.
- **Why:** the game knows for sure that the fingers sit on the right keys while the one finger reaches.
  It cannot be typed with "wrong" fingers.
- **Keyboard limit:** not every keyboard reports many keys at once. Laptop and cheap keyboards often lose
  a third or fourth key in some combinations, and which ones depends on the keyboard. So:
  - before the first such step, a short check: hold the keys, tap the next one, and see what came in;
  - if the keyboard cannot do it, the step asks for fewer held keys (down to one), and never blames the
    child;
  - the result is kept per device, not per child.
- **Safety:** a held key counts as let go when the window loses focus, and repeated key events from
  holding are ignored.

## 3. Up and down one finger's column (P2)
- **Idea:** one finger goes from home up and down its own column and back, for example `jum`, `fvr`,
  `ki,`, and on the Slovak number row `ľ`, `š`, `č`.
- **Why:** it trains the reach, not new keys.
- **Games:** it fits a world where the third or fourth key of a column comes:
  - the keys move a magnet or crane up and down to catch or lift things;
  - music notes fly higher or lower to pass obstacles;
  - an elevator stops on the floor of the typed key.

## 4. Levels that need exact keys (P2)
- **Today:** a level opens when the child knows enough keys (a count). The key order is fixed per
  layout, but the count does not promise exact keys: own key choice, "no diacritics" and other layouts
  give other sets.
- **Idea:** a level may also say which keys (or which finger and row) it needs, so a level built on, say,
  the left index column can count on those keys. Keys by finger and row work for every layout.
- **Open question:** what the map shows when the count is reached but a needed key is missing (for
  example "Learn R first" with a way to that key).

## 5. Smaller ideas (P2-P3)
- **Tricky words:** a sentence first, then the word a few times. Fits Slovak *vybrané slová* (`byť/biť`,
  `mi/my`, `výr/vír`) and `ä`, `ô`, `ĺ`, `ŕ`.
- **Common letter groups:** `nie`, `ova`, `pre`, `sta`, `ch`, `ostí` typed as one movement.
- **Texts that teach while typed:** a typing tip is itself the text to type ("Feel the bumps on F and J").
- **Rhymes as a word set:** `mak, rak, pták`, light fun with the same keys.
