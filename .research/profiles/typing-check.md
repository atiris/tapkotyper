# Typing check and personal certificate

## What
- Once the letters (and later sentences) are learned, a calm "typing check" opens every few
  weeks: one minute of the same kind of text, no game objects, no visible timer for the
  child (only a soft end).
- The result card shows accuracy first, then words per minute, and the child's earlier
  checks as a small line ("in March 9 words, today 16 words"). No grades, no comparison
  with other children or with "normal for your age".
- The child can print the newest result as a certificate with Tapko and the date.

## Why
- Certificates are a loved part of typing sites; reviewers say certificates and speed and
  accuracy challenges encourage students to keep improving.
- Parents ask "is my child getting better?"; the current trends come from mixed game play
  (different levels, difficulty), so they are hard to compare over months. A fixed check
  gives a fair number.
- Users dislike typing tests that only allow a fixed time to complete the text - hide the timer
  for children.

## Fit
- Speed appears, but only against the child's own past and after accuracy, as in the
  existing feedback principle.
- Builds on the existing printable progress card.
- Risk: a child may feel tested; it is optional and can be repeated any time.

## Priority
P3, effort S.
