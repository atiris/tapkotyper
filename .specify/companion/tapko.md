# The character (Tapko)

## What
- The child plays **as** a cute animal character shown on screen during play: the character
  is the child, not a friend beside them. "Tapko" is only the game's name for the figure;
  in play the character carries the child's **nickname**, or the child's name when the
  nickname is empty. The child picks the animal kind when creating the profile (free
  choice); a parent can change nickname and kind in the settings. Kinds: fox (default),
  cat, bunny, owl, dog, bear, panda, penguin, lion. All kinds share one front-view body
  shape, so every accessory fits every kind.
- A friendly **narrator** speaks to the child in the second person ("you") in the speech
  bubble: it encourages on mistakes (never blames), celebrates successes, gives hints and
  leads the breaks. The character never talks about itself, and no text talks about the
  character as someone other than the child. Messages come from a localized pool plus the
  parent's own custom encouraging messages. Slovak texts never use a gendered past tense
  or gendered adjectives about the child, whose gender the game does not know.
- Accessories unlock by progress (finishing levels and worlds, mastering keys): hats,
  glasses, scarves and bows, vests and shirts, wings, capes and backpacks, shoes, things to
  hold (wand, balloon, lantern, a walking stick), gloves, necklaces (a chain, a medallion
  on a cord), hair clips, a simple eye mask and a festive carnival mask. Story 2 adds a hard hat, safety goggles, a raincoat, an
  umbrella worn as a hat, a small jetpack and an antenna headband. Which level, world or
  mastery step gives which accessory is **random per player, fixed from the start**, and
  the wardrobe shows how to earn each one. The same name always gives the same layout;
  different names give different ones. Accessories a story scene names stay on their
  place for everyone: the flower wreath (world 1), the umbrella (world 8), the raincoat
  (a level of world 9), the feather (world 12), the harp (world 14) and the fairy wings
  (world 15). One item per body area (head, eyes, neck, body, back, hand, feet); the child
  mixes them freely in the "Me and my wardrobe" screen, for a time earned by practice
  ([wardrobe time](wardrobe.md)).
- In the same screen the child sets the nickname (up to 16 characters, may be empty) and
  picks another animal kind. Other kinds are earned by stars over all levels, slowly:
  about one new animal per two worlds. Every player gets their own order and prices, set
  at random when the player is created: each next kind costs 18 to 26 stars more than the
  previous one, so the last costs 144 to 208 of the 234 possible stars; the starting kind
  is free. The screen's header shows the stars the child has now, so the missing stars
  are easy to compare. Every tile shows the kind's name on one line; a locked kind is paler,
  with a lock, and its tooltip says how many stars are missing ("62 more ★ and Lion is
  yours"); a new
  character is announced on the level results. Accessories stay on when the kind changes.
  A back item can have a front part (a cape's clasp, a backpack's straps) that lies over
  the body while the item itself stays behind.
- No fixed curious questions lure the child to the next level: children did not care, playing
  is reason enough. Only a parent's surprises ([parent controls](../profiles/parent-controls.md))
  ask a question and tell its answer.
- **Paw help**: fifteen words in a row without a mistake charge one paw (three at most,
  kept between levels). In story levels and hero's journeys (not at the main gate, whose
  code must be the child's own), Enter (or the round paw button next to pause: a paw in
  the character's color, ringed by three arcs for the charges; its label names Enter; with no
  charge it keeps its colors, only its arcs are empty, never dimmed like a disabled button)
  sends the character's paws onto the keyboard in place of the hands, and the paw types
  the three closest words with its own rhythm. These keys never count in the child's
  statistics, accuracy or rhythm, and the child's own keys wait meanwhile. The paw comes
  only when the child calls it: there is no automatic paw, and Backspace does nothing in a
  level (only in [free typing](../game/free-typing.md)). A target waiting for its Enter
  takes the Enter instead of calling the paw.
- Everything the character says in a level is in one speech bubble with one fixed spot at
  the top left of the scene, slightly see-through, without a tail: the character moves and
  can be anywhere, so the bubble points at nobody and does not move with the jumps. It never
  covers a word: when it would, it hides until the spot is free.
- Clicking the character, on the map or in a level, opens the progress and statistics
  overlay ([statistics](../profiles/statistics.md)); its title says "Click yourself to see
  how you are doing".
- On the egg mountain (world 5, levels 1-4) the character carries the dragon egg on its back:
  it is drawn behind everything the character wears and always peeks out, at least about a
  third of it at rest, whatever the outfit; it rises to peek now and then and at story moments.
- After world 5 a newly hatched friend (a baby dragon) joins; in story 2 the robot Bolt
  walks next to the child, in story 3 the fairy Melody floats next to the child
  ([story](../game/story.md)).
- The character needs no new base pictures for stories 2 and 3; an optional "surprised"
  face is used only in the meeting scene with Bolt (else the happy one). Story 3 rewards
  are a feather, a little harp and fairy wings.

## Why
- A child who plays as the fox wants the fox to carry their nickname; texts that talk
  about the fox as another person break that.
- Gentle, personal feedback replaces punishment and makes the child's progress visible
  and their own.
- Slow, per-player character prices keep a reason to collect stars through all three
  stories, and different prices and accessories per player make siblings compare and
  talk about what they got.
- The word is the child's task, so it must always be readable.
- Paw help is a reward for accuracy that makes play more fun and takes the edge off a
  crowded moment, without blurring what the child really typed.
- The paw stays on Enter alone: Backspace is the key a child presses to fix a typo, so it
  must never change a setting or start help the child did not ask for.
