# Help

## What
- One help page for everything, in short, big, friendly text (sk and en), in tabs like the
  settings screen (the tab row right above its panel), closed with the round X at the top right
  or with Escape:
  1. **Interface**: the buttons of the map and of a level, each drawn exactly as it looks in the
     game with one line on what it does, and the player's card on the map (the companion opens
     "My progress", the side buttons, the task card).
  2. **How to type**: sitting, the home keys F and J, thumbs over the space bar, each finger
     with its own keys, eyes on the screen, accuracy before speed, short breaks; and how a level
     is played (type the word on the object, nothing bad happens when it gets away, stars come
     from accuracy, Esc pauses).
  3. **How to progress**, in the child's voice: stories, worlds and levels, stars, letters that
     open levels and stories, "My progress" with its three tabs and QR codes, rescue missions
     and badges, a task from a teacher.
  4. **Settings**, for the parent: what each settings tab does (child, practice, look and sound,
     password, own words and text, surprises, tasks, keys, progress, data), with one section per
     settings tab, and what is lost without a backup.
  5. **About TapkoTyper**: the intro, the privacy promise, data and the visit counter with its
     live count, how it teaches, links, the code and a bug report.
- The tab is part of the address (`#/p/<id>/help/<tab>`, `#/help/<tab>`), so a tab can be linked.
- The round "?" in the map's top buttons opens it on the interface tab. The "?" on the start
  page (and on the other screens before a player is chosen) opens it on the about tab; help
  works without a player, the companion is then called Tapko.
- A "Help" button in a level's pause card opens it over the paused level, on how to type; closing
  it returns to the paused level.
- A purple round "?" button in a level's top bar pauses the level and opens a card: what to do
  now (the level's intro and one tip), how to do best (accuracy first, then a steady rhythm,
  the paw when stuck, repeat for three stars, rest after a few levels) and the real buttons.
  Escape or "Continue" closes it and resumes.
- Works on a 1024x600 screen; the panel scrolls, the title, the tabs and the X stay in sight.
- Explains the paw (what it shows, when to press), the ring with the level's number beside the pause button (levels done, this one, to come, a web for the big challenge), the rescue mission and the badge, each in one short sentence. The level help card has the same text, left-aligned and without the super star.

## Why
- Children do not guess what symbols mean, and the parent is not always next to them.
- One place to look: the child, the parent and the teacher find every explanation behind the
  same "?", instead of a help page and a separate about page.
- Parents rarely read settings hints one by one; a page that explains them all lets them set the
  game up once with confidence.
- Opening help from a level must not end the run, so the level only waits paused.
