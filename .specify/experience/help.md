# Help

## What
- One help page for everything, in short, big, friendly text (sk and en), in tabs like the
  settings screen (the tab row right above its panel). It is a page like the settings: "Back"
  at the top left in the same top bar that stays while the page scrolls, the same title style,
  and the text in a column that keeps the same distance from the window's sides as the back button has from the left edge, up to about 1200 px on a very wide screen (only a narrow window wraps the tabs);
  on a wide desktop the bar lists the tabs once they scrolled away. Escape goes back too.
  Back returns to where help was opened (settings without asking the parent question again),
  else to the map, or to the start page before a player is chosen. Opened from the settings,
  help starts on the settings tab:
  1. **Interface**: the buttons of the map and of a level, each drawn exactly as it looks in the
     game with one line on what it does, and the player's card on the map (the companion opens
     "My progress", the side buttons, the task card).
  2. **Typing**: sitting, the home keys F and J, thumbs over the space bar, each finger
     with its own keys, eyes on the screen, accuracy before speed, short breaks.
  3. **Progress**, in the child's voice: stories, worlds and levels, stars, how a level is
     played (type the word on the object, nothing bad happens when it gets away, stars come from
     accuracy, Esc pauses), the kinds of games by their standard names with one line each, each
     with the picture the map shows for it (the target range's target, the parrot and the big
     challenge's crown too; the same names as the map dots' tooltips; a new kind joins the list
     when it is in the game; Transcribe is listed ahead of its levels, Ribbon names its first level, the spell on the egg mountain's summit) right above letters that open levels and stories, "My progress" (a page from the map's
     button with four tabs, a quick look by a click on the character, the parrots, QR codes and
     the certificate), the hero's journey, the target range (where it opens, what it
     trains, the middle, a fall that costs nothing, the collection and Enter), the parrot (where it
     opens, why it repeats words, parrots that fly away and the Parrot badge) and badges.
  4. **Settings**, for the teacher or parent: under the Settings heading, that they are for the teacher or
     parent, open with the gear, and until locked with a password open for anyone by one confirm button, what each settings tab does (child, practice, sounds and interface,
     password, own words and text, surprises, tasks, keys, data and the translator), with one section per
     settings tab, and what is lost without a backup. By the password, plainly: it guards the
     settings only inside the game; the data in the browser is not encrypted and anyone with
     direct access can read or change it, and anyone using the computer can clear the site's
     data, which removes every player, the progress, the results and the password.
  5. **Rodičia a učitelia** (Parents and teachers), in one place: the "For teachers" page, scanning
     QR codes by group, result links, sets (one scan, or links within 20 minutes), the kept
     history of every hand-in and a writer's progress, sending results from a phone to a
     computer as a `.tapko` file, the yearly cleanup (7 more days for old results loaded from a
     file), CSV, the checksum, making a task, the task from the child's side, and the accuracy each count
     of stars needs (the numbers from the game itself, so they never drift). The "?" (and F1) on the
     "For teachers" page opens this tab, since a teacher there needs exactly this.
  6. **About Tapko**: the intro, the privacy promise (its word "nainštalujete" is a link that asks the browser to install
     the web app, or opens the browser's guide when it cannot), data and one bullet with the open
     GoatCounter address (we hide nothing: anyone sees the same usage data we do) and the visits
     recorded so far, how it teaches, links, the code and a bug report. Links are in the app's
     orange, underlined only on hover.
- The tab is part of the address (`#/p/<id>/help/<tab>`, `#/help/<tab>`), so a tab can be linked.
- The round "?" in the map's top buttons opens it on the interface tab. The "?" on the start
  page (and on the other screens before a player is chosen) opens it on the about tab; help
  works without a player and names no one.
- **Help in a level**: the purple round "?" in the level's top bar and
  F1 pause the level and open the same help page over the whole level, opaque, with "Back" at
  the top left. It opens on how to type, with "In this level" first: the level's intro and one
  tip. Back or Escape returns to the paused level, on "Continue"; the level is not lost.
- **F1** opens the help that fits the moment everywhere: on the map the interface tab, in the
  settings the settings tab, on "My progress" (the page or the quick look) how to progress, on the start page about.
  The help tab of the interface lists the keyboard keys (Enter and space, Tab and arrows, the
  player numbers and nickname, F1-F4 and F8, Esc).
- Works on a 1024x600 screen; the page scrolls and the bar with Back stays in sight.
- A few key points per tab are in bold (about a dozen in all, a phrase, never a whole
  sentence): the home keys, Esc pauses, stars from accuracy, the paw's Enter, the parent's
  password, results sent as a link or code, progress kept only in this browser.
- Explains that on a small screen the top buttons hide under one Menu button, and (in how to
  type and in about) that a real keyboard is needed, also on a phone or tablet.
- Explains the wardrobe's countdown (a smile, then a number that drops every 10 seconds, blinking at the end, a lock when the time is up, time earned by practice) next to the map's buttons.
- Explains the paw (what it shows, when to press Enter; no other key calls it, Backspace neither), the ring with the level's number beside the pause button (filled up to this level, full at the big challenge), the hero's journey and the badge, each in one short sentence.

- **Ms Turtle's tips**: with "Introduce the app's features" on (the default), a panel above the
  worlds on the map now and then shows one thing about the app, from Ms Turtle the teacher (three
  pictures of her taking turns from tip to tip: with a raised finger and a pointer, sitting with
  a book and waving, holding a glowing light bulb and pointing to the right; no speech bubble). It is the only panel
  above the worlds. The first two come one right after the other and introduce the app: hello
  by nickname, the character on the left is the one the child picked for this typing adventure,
  with whom they go through several stories and many worlds; then what a click on the character
  shows (how much was typed, accuracy and speed, which keys go well and which need practice,
  the exercises from the teacher). Then where help is, that the whole app works by keyboard, My progress, a task from the
  teacher and sending its result back, sharing results, the target range and the parrot. Each
  tip comes once and only when it fits (the task tip only with a task, sharing after a few
  levels); the first four at most one an hour, the rest one every day and a half; none with
  the install offer, never in a level. OK, the round X or Esc read it; a tip may
  have a second button that leads to where it is told more (a help tab, My progress), which
  reads it too.

## Why
- Children do not guess what symbols mean, and the parent is not always next to them.
- Much of the app is out of sight (My progress behind Tapko, sharing, tasks and their result
  links, the keyboard shortcuts); one short tip at a time, when it fits and rarely, shows it
  without a manual and without nagging.
- One place to look: the child, the parent and the teacher find every explanation behind the
  same "?", instead of a help page and a separate about page.
- Parents rarely read settings hints one by one; a page that explains them all lets them set the
  game up once with confidence.
- Opening help from a level must not end the run, so the level only waits paused. A
  see-through help over a busy level was hard to read, so it is a whole page.
- Help that opens where you are and goes back there keeps the child or parent in their task;
  bold key points make plain help text easy to scan.
