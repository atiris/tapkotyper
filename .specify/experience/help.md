# Help

## What
- One help page for everything, in short, big, friendly text (sk and en), in tabs like the
  settings screen (the tab row right above its panel). It is a page like the settings: "Back"
  at the top left in the same top bar that stays while the page scrolls, the same title style,
  and the text in a narrower column (wide enough for all tabs in one row, about 840 px, wider on a wide screen, up to about 1040 px; only a narrower window wraps the tabs);
  on a wide desktop the bar lists the tabs once they scrolled away. Escape goes back too.
  Back returns to where help was opened (settings without asking the parent question again),
  else to the map, or to the start page before a player is chosen. Opened from the settings,
  help starts on the settings tab:
  1. **Interface**: the buttons of the map and of a level, each drawn exactly as it looks in the
     game with one line on what it does, and the player's card on the map (the companion opens
     "My progress", the side buttons, the task card).
  2. **Typing**: sitting, the home keys F and J, thumbs over the space bar, each finger
     with its own keys, eyes on the screen, accuracy before speed, short breaks.
  3. **Progress**, in the child's voice: stories, worlds and levels, stars, how a level is
     played (type the word on the object, nothing bad happens when it gets away, stars come from
     accuracy, Esc pauses) right above letters that open levels and stories, "My progress" with
     its three tabs and QR codes, rescue missions, the target range (where it opens, what it
     trains, the middle, a fall that costs nothing, the collection and Enter) and badges.
  4. **Settings**, for the teacher or parent: that the settings open with the gear on the map (the child's
     screens do not say it), what each settings tab does (child, practice, look and sound,
     password, own words and text, surprises, tasks, keys, progress, data), with one section per
     settings tab, and what is lost without a backup. By the password, plainly: it guards the
     settings only inside the game; the data in the browser is not encrypted and anyone with
     direct access can read or change it, and anyone using the computer can clear the site's
     data, which removes every player, the progress, the results and the password.
  5. **For teachers and parents**, in one place: the "For teachers" page, scanning
     QR codes by group, result links, sets (one scan, or links within 20 minutes), the kept
     history of every hand-in and a writer's progress, sending results from a phone to a
     computer as a `.tapko` file, the yearly cleanup (7 more days for old results loaded from a
     file), CSV, the checksum, making a task, and the task from the child's side. The "?" (and F1) on the
     "For teachers" page opens this tab, since a teacher there needs exactly this.
  6. **About Tapko**: the intro, the privacy promise, data and one bullet with the open
     GoatCounter address (we hide nothing: anyone sees the same usage data we do) and the visits
     recorded so far, how it teaches, links, the code and a bug report. Links are in the app's
     orange, underlined only on hover.
- The tab is part of the address (`#/p/<id>/help/<tab>`, `#/help/<tab>`), so a tab can be linked.
- The round "?" in the map's top buttons opens it on the interface tab. The "?" on the start
  page (and on the other screens before a player is chosen) opens it on the about tab; help
  works without a player and names no one.
- **Help in a level**: the purple round "?" in the level's top bar, "Help" on the pause card and
  F1 pause the level and open the same help page over the whole level, opaque, with "Back" at
  the top left. It opens on how to type, with "In this level" first: the level's intro and one
  tip. Back or Escape returns to the paused level, on "Continue"; the level is not lost.
- **F1** opens the help that fits the moment everywhere: on the map the interface tab, in the
  settings the settings tab, with "My progress" open how to progress, on the start page about.
  The help tab of the interface lists the keyboard keys (Enter and space, Tab and arrows, the
  player numbers and nickname, F1-F4 and F8, Esc).
- Works on a 1024x600 screen; the page scrolls and the bar with Back stays in sight.
- A few key points per tab are in bold (about a dozen in all, a phrase, never a whole
  sentence): the home keys, Esc pauses, stars from accuracy, the paw's Enter, the parent's
  password, results sent as a link or code, progress kept only in this browser.
- Explains that on a small screen the top buttons hide under one Menu button, and (in how to
  type and in about) that a real keyboard is needed, also on a phone or tablet.
- Explains the wardrobe's countdown (a smile, then a number that drops every 10 seconds, blinking at the end, time earned by practice) next to the map's buttons.
- Explains the paw (what it shows, when to press Enter; no other key calls it, Backspace neither), the ring with the level's number beside the pause button (filled up to this level, full at the big challenge), the rescue mission and the badge, each in one short sentence.

## Why
- Children do not guess what symbols mean, and the parent is not always next to them.
- One place to look: the child, the parent and the teacher find every explanation behind the
  same "?", instead of a help page and a separate about page.
- Parents rarely read settings hints one by one; a page that explains them all lets them set the
  game up once with confidence.
- Opening help from a level must not end the run, so the level only waits paused. A
  see-through help over a busy level was hard to read, so it is a whole page.
- Help that opens where you are and goes back there keeps the child or parent in their task;
  bold key points make plain help text easy to scan.
