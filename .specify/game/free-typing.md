# Just typing

## What
- A third way to type beside own practice and "My text" on the map: **Just typing**. No
  levels, targets, stars, pace or end; the child types whatever they like.
- The child picks the place among the worlds already open (their map pictures); with one
  open world it starts there at once. The place can be changed while typing, and the
  place picker only offers open worlds.
- Tapko walks through the chosen world (its background and music). He starts in the bottom
  left corner, where he stands in other levels, so the bubble at the top left reads as his;
  from there he strolls on the ground into the left part and back. Every key makes him
  hop happily with a small sound and a few sparkles; with reduced motion he only smiles.
- The typed characters collect in a quiet line at the top. After about 1.5 seconds without
  a key (fixed, not a setting), or at once with Enter, the text is shown as a word or short
  phrase above Tapko for a few seconds, and the line is cleared. A full line (32 characters)
  is shown as soon as the next key comes. Backspace removes the last typed character.
  While nothing is typed or shown, the line says what to do.
- The text is read aloud when reading aloud is on, the browser has a voice for the
  language, and the parent setting "Read typed words aloud" allows it (on by default); with
  it off the reading-aloud button is hidden here. Screen readers hear the shown text.
- Only keys the layout types count (letters, digits, space, punctuation, diacritics).
  Free typing has no task, so it cannot tell good typing from mashing one or two keys:
  nothing typed here counts in the statistics (no key presses, readiness, speed, accuracy,
  training time or wardrobe time), and Tapko says so in the bubble at the start. The time
  counts only toward the break reminder: when a break is due, the narrator says so in the bubble (there is no end screen). It never gives stars,
  levels, keys or task time.
- No game cards: no get-ready card, no new key, no tip, no paw help. Esc or the round X
  leaves to the map; the keyboard guide can be shown as in other levels.
- The typed text is never kept in the profile, the results or exported files.

## Why
- Children want to type their own name and silly words, not only what the game asks; a
  calm sandbox with a happy reaction to every key makes the keyboard itself the toy.
- Showing and saying the word turns typing into writing: the child sees that keys make
  words that mean something.
- Reading typed words aloud can be misused for rude words. The voice runs only in this
  browser and nothing is stored or sent, so it is no worse than typing the word in any
  text field. A word filter is not used: lists of forbidden words are language-specific,
  always incomplete and block innocent words; the parent switch is the honest control.
- Free play has no "wrong" key, so any number it gave would be made up: ten minutes of
  "ajajaj" would look like fast, perfect typing and could open new keys or earn wardrobe
  time. Leaving it out keeps the statistics honest, and saying so keeps the child from
  expecting progress here.
