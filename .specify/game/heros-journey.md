# Hero's journey

## What
- **Cesta hrdinu** / **Hero's journey**: one long, harder trip that closes each story. Its
  symbol is the **compass**, the picture of its map node.
- It sits in the card of the story's last world, right after the crown of its super level
  and before the target range and the parrot. It opens with that world done, the same moment
  as the range and the parrot; before that the node is there, faded, with the tooltip "opens
  when the world is finished".
- It plays one part for every world of the story, in world order. Each part is the game of
  that world's first level, on that world's background. A story may define its own journey
  instead. A world not built yet plays its placeholder ([story](story.md)).
- Only the get-ready card comes before the first part. Between parts a panel on the card of every
  end ([mechanics](mechanics.md)): "Cesta hrdinu pokračuje!" with the round X; on the left the map
  picture of the world the next part goes to, on the right the next part's own story instead of
  stats. Buttons: "Ideme ďalej" (focused; the X, Esc, Space and Enter too), "Krátke cvičenie" (the
  pause card's short stretch, then the panel again) and "Vzdať to" last. No other intro cards and
  no key cards.
- Leaving a journey, from the panel's "Vzdať to" or the pause card's "Ukončiť písanie", asks one
  question: "Hrdina, vydrž to!", that the journey is a tough nut even for the best heroes, to try
  to finish or come back later, and that stopping now it does not count and starts from the
  beginning next time. "Pokračovať v ceste" is the safe, focused choice (Esc too), "Ukončiť
  písanie" the other.
- It lasts three times the minimum of a regular level of its story, split evenly over the parts;
  the planned words likewise ([mechanics](mechanics.md)). Every part plays at least its share;
  a part whose own count would run out early gets more of its units instead of ending.
- The progress bar shows the whole journey: each part fills only its own slice (after the first of
  five parts it stands at a fifth).
- The play is harder, and the get-ready card says so in one line: the difficulty is one step
  above the child's (at most 10), the words are harder (a letter longer, and longer phrases),
  and three stars need 98 % accuracy.
- Stars come from the accuracy of the whole journey, one step stricter than a level: 3 stars
  from 98 %, 2 from 95 %, else 1. The card shows halves: 3 from 98 %, 2.5 from 96.5 %, 2 from
  95 %, 1.5 from 90 %, else 1. The paw rule (fewer than 5 own keys gives one star) holds over
  the whole journey.
- It counts like a level: its keys, time and rhythm go into the [statistics](../profiles/statistics.md),
  and growth and the lesson advance as after a story level. The best stars show as mini stars
  on its node and add to the star total (234).
- It is not a level of its world: it gives no badge, does not finish the world and opens
  nothing. The parent's surprises count it as a level of the story's last world
  ([parent controls](../profiles/parent-controls.md)).
- The end card (the one card of every end, see [mechanics](mechanics.md)) is "Cesta hrdinu
  prejdená!" with the compass, the stars, accuracy, rhythm and minutes. Buttons: "Hrať znova" and
  the map. Staying a while is not offered.

## The dragon story's journey
- The egg has hatched on the summit. Tapko takes the **little dragon** (the hatched baby, out
  of its shell) back **down from the Mountain of Awakening to his rocket** and takes off. The
  little dragon walks and flutters right behind Tapko in every part, never a target: glad at a
  word and at a finished part, worried at a slip or a stumble. In the flight it is the one
  flying, and at the take-off it climbs into the rocket after Tapko.
- The get-ready card tells the whole way: the little dragon hatched and stays, down from the
  Mountain of Awakening through the Dragons' Valley, the Crystal Meadow and the swamp to the
  rocket waiting on the shore for its crystal fuel, and the little stones rolling down; then the
  line about the harder play. Each panel between parts tells the next part's goal. Its picture is
  the next part's world; the take-off shows the rocket world's (it is the way into space), though
  it plays on the planet's shore.
- The parts, in the order of the way home (Tapko always goes to the right, as everywhere):
  1. **Down the mountain** (the mountain's bands): the small stones of the mountain's fourth
     level roll after him, exactly as there; a missed one makes the way longer by the part's own
     share, at most ten times. A slip in a stone's word brings one more stone and one planned
     word's time, once a stone, at most seven times.
  2. **Through the valley** (the valley's bands, the burning pit on the right): the little
     dragon learns to fly over the two bushes, always between Tapko and the pit: to the pit,
     back to Tapko, and on. Only a flight that lands where it aimed counts; one that lands in the
     bushes or hard walks back and is flown again the same way, a little easier each time. It
     needs two good flights, and more while the part's minimum is not nearly played. A failed
     flight costs no stars.
  3. **The crystal meadow** (the planet's meadow): the crystals of the planet's second level,
     the energy gems the rocket needs; it needs at least seven, and the end says how many it
     gets. A second slip in a crystal's word cracks it at once. The four valley dragons sit at
     the back of the meadow (they flew here at the valley's take-off) and wave.
  4. **The swamp** (the planet's swamp): the great swamp's fragile stones (three stones and the
     firm fourth, a slip cracks one and he goes back), without the log and the monolith, at
     least four sets before the shore. The little dragon flies, hovering over Tapko's head. It
     ends on the shore, where the rocket stands ready on the grass.
  5. **The take-off** (the same shore): the rocket world's command game on the ground. The
     rocket stands well away from the water, Tapko right beside it. It accepts at least seven
     commands, each answered with a short "Potvrdzujem!", "Rozumiem!" or the like, the last one
     with the countdown; a rejected one is answered "Chyba!", "Preklep!" or the like and nothing
     happens. After the last one Tapko and the little dragon climb in, "3, 2, 1, štart!", and the
     rocket rises off the top of the stage with its flame and smoke.
- Counted parts keep their own units scaled to the part: the flight counts its good flights.
- The end card is the journey's; the first finish says in its green line "Raketa letí domov aj
  s dráčikom!".

## Why
- A second, weaker reward loop beside the story is not needed. What is worth having is one
  final test that closes a story.
- A long, harder trip through all the worlds of the story lets the child show how much they
  learned; counting like a real level makes it matter for stars and growth.
- Stricter stars give a goal for children who finished the story with ease, and the get-ready
  card tells the difficulty up front, so it is never a surprise.
- Breaking the length into one part per world keeps it varied and short enough in each part;
  the panel between parts gives a moment to breathe (or stretch) and says what comes next. Its
  look is the end card's, so the journey feels like one trip with stops, not five levels.
- The bar over the whole journey shows how far the child is; a bar that filled in each part
  promised an end that was not there.
- A whole trip of about two levels felt too short for the final test of a story; three levels'
  length, and parts that never end before their share, make it a real journey.
- Giving up is offered, but the question encourages finishing first: the journey is meant to be
  hard, and leaving it loses everything played.
- It does not open anything and is not a level of its world, so a child never needs it to move
  on in the story.
- Each part of the dragon story's journey asks for a goal of its own (two good flights, seven
  crystals, four sets of stones, seven commands): the story makes sense only when the little
  dragon really learned to fly and the rocket really got its fuel. A failed flight gets easier
  each time, so no child is stuck in the valley.
- The dragon story's journey is the story's own ending: the hatched dragon goes home, and the
  way back passes the story's key places in the order of the trip, so it reads as one journey,
  not five replays. World 1's meadow is Tapko's home, not on this planet, so the planet comes
  twice (its meadow and its swamp), and the shore where the swamp ends is where the rocket
  waits.
