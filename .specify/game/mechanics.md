# Mechanics

## What
- Targets (creatures, rocks, signs) carry a word. Typing its first letter locks onto it,
  further letters fill it in, and completing the word resolves the target peacefully.
- A wrong letter never unlocks the target or resets progress on the word. Two levels of the egg
  mountain are the exceptions, each told on its card: a slip in a gust of wind starts its word
  again, and a slip in the spell ribbon erases the word in progress (see Ribbon).
- Backspace wipes the started word and lets go of it, so the child who started the wrong one
  of several words can start any of them again. It is no mistake and costs nothing.
- Target speed and count follow difficulty (1-10) and the child's word pace, so the game
  stays calm for slow typists. The word pace is the time from a word appearing to its last
  letter, per letter, over the last five words the child typed: reading the word and
  finding the first key count, not only the rhythm inside the word. Every word also gets
  a few seconds to be read. With "no time pressure", targets wait for the child.
- When two targets pass by untyped in a row, new targets come slower (up to twice the
  time); every three typed in a row bring the pace one step back. This is silent and lasts
  only for the level.
- Progress comes only from typing. A target that floats by untyped (a goal, a splash) is
  replaced by a new one; waiting never finishes a level. Timed own practice is the one
  chosen duration.
- A goal text appears above the animal that scored. A closed world on the map tells, when
  clicked, what is still missing: the world before it, or how many letters its story needs.
- Automatic growth counts only levels where the child typed at least 85 % of the targets
  (and typed accurately); a level where many targets floated by unread adds no time. The time it
  adds is typing time ([statistics](../profiles/statistics.md)), so long reading or waiting delays
  growth a little.
- A small round rhythm gauge in the top bar (calm blue to green, never red; in the space world
  also the rocket's speedometer, without a caption) shows the current smooth typing rhythm, not raw WPM.
- A word the child can see can be typed at once; a target that cannot be typed yet shows
  no word.
- A word label is always wholly in sight with its bubble: a word near an edge of the stage moves
  inward (an object coming in from the right shows its word only once it is in sight).
- Word labels never overlap: the most urgent word keeps its place, the others glide aside
  (up, or down near the top), and a far-moved word gets a thin line to its object.
- The start is slow: at difficulty 3 a short word needs about 10 seconds to reach Tapko
  and one word is on screen at a time.
- Every story level, super level lasts a minimum of active play that grows
  with the story: 2 minutes in the dragon story, 3 in the robot story, 4 in the fairy story.
  Pauses and cards do not count, nor waiting over a word: once a word waits and no key came for
  5 seconds, the clock stops until the next key, so a break in typing or words left to fly by
  never fill the minimum (the paws' help and the mode's own scenes without a word count). A calm step lasts a quarter less, a super level a quarter
  more; a hero's journey lasts twice a regular level of its story, split evenly over its parts. Even 4 minutes is well under the 10-15 minutes a day
  usually recommended for children and under a KTouch lesson (5-10 minutes): a level is one part
  of a session, not the whole of it.
- A level also asks for a minimum of words: what a slow but practising child types in the
  minimum time, 8 / 10 / 12 words a minute in the three stories (children of 7-9 type about
  5-15 words a minute, 10-20 with practice). A level plans that many targets in its own unit
  (a big asteroid, a dragon, a copy target count as the words they hold). A quick child gets
  more targets of the same kind until the minimum time is played, never a long wait with
  nothing to type; a slow child at that pace gets no more than the minimum needs.
- **Story moments in a level** (a log to wait for, a boulder, a gust) are planned against the
  same two players: the quick one and the slow one at the story's pace. A moment that takes
  words costs a share of the level's planned words, not a fixed number, and it comes only while
  the minimum's time still holds the words to reach it and finish it. Each moment the story
  promises appears for both players (a slow child may meet fewer repeats, never none), and a
  test plays the level at both paces and checks that it appears.
- **The stage's width changes during play** (the on-screen keyboard hidden or shown, the window
  resized, fullscreen on or off; the height stays). Everything placed by the stage's width (rings
  on a path, a target, Tapko by the edge) keeps its place in the scene's own terms (a share of the
  path, a share of the width) and follows the new width at once; nothing keeps the place it had
  when it came.
  The valley's flight levels count dragons (three, one more while the minimum is not played,
  at most five) and the fifth ends the level even below the minimum ([story](story.md)).
  Two mountain levels make the way longer: a gust that rushes into Tapko after a second slip
  adds 2 planned words and their time at the story's pace (at most 10 words in a level), and a
  missed boulder adds 7 % of the level's planned words and of its minimum (at most 70 %).
  The progress bar shows the slower of the two shares, the targets done
  and the minimum time played, so it never runs ahead of either, ends exactly when the level
  ends and never goes back when more targets come. Practice,
  custom exercises and free typing keep their own length. At the end Tapko stays where he is and jumps happily
  three times (Bolt hops with him in story 2, Melody hops in story 3; a super level adds a cheer), and any tool he held in the level (gloves,
  torch, umbrella) fades away; with reduced motion he only smiles and the tool is gone
  at once. About 1.4 seconds later the end card comes (see The end card). Accuracy counts only the child's own keys: a level where the child
  typed fewer than 5 keys (the paw did the rest) says so in a yellow tip line, gives one star, never
  lowers a better result and adds no time toward automatic growth. Esc on the end screen
  peeks under it and a second Esc brings it back (see Peeking under the results).
- **Stars**: 3 from 96 % accuracy, 2 from 89 %, else 1 (finishing always gives one). The
  valley's flying lessons also count the dragons that flew: never more stars than they earn. The end
  screen also shows half stars, 1.5 from 84 % and 2.5 from 93 %, so a child sees a small gain
  before the next whole star; the map, the progress table and every count keep whole stars
  only, the shown stars rounded down.
- **World rhythm** (the meadow, the rocket world and the valley; the planet starts with its calm step, see [story](story.md)): the levels of a world have fixed roles, so one
  session (about one world) goes up and down instead of repeating one game: 1 the world's
  main game, gently; 2 a calm step; 3 the main game, harder, with one short surprise; 4 a
  twist; 5 the super-obstacle. Each level dot on the map shows the icon of its activity
  (main game a flying leaf, waiting words a chair, copy an envelope, crossroads a signpost,
  the super level a crown)
  (the number stays in its tooltip and for screen readers). The super level's crown, the
  target range's target and the parrot sit on white cards until they are mastered, then turn
  green like every played level: the crown once the super level is done, the target once the
  world's own thing is found at the range, the parrot once its round was won (it stays green
  with the empty perch after the parrot flew away). Their pictures are in colour and faded like
  any level not reached yet, and only the crown has stars under it.
  - *Waiting words* (calm step): still objects carry short words (2 letters in the meadow),
    each starting with another letter, so the first key picks the word and every later key
    is measured against the key it should be; a key that starts no word counts only in the
    level's accuracy (in the rocket world the meteors the astronaut hops across carry words of
    up to 4 letters). New words keep coming until the minimum time is played. Nothing moves
    toward the child and nothing is missed. A calm step lasts a quarter less than the
    story's minimum.
  - *Copy* (twist, a clean line): a row of 3 to 4 words typed from left to right. A slip turns the
    word red, it fades to a faint trace (about a fifth) where it stood and the next word is the one to type; the level gets 2 more
    words, at most (1 + story order) x 3 in all (6 in the dragon story).
  - *Ribbon* (the egg mountain's super level, the first of its kind): one long text runs as an
    endless band in a wide word box: the previous word faint, the word to type with its typed
    letters marked, the next word faint, fading out at both ends; it slides letter by letter so
    the next letter stays at one point, and a space is a gap that lights up when it is the key.
    Spaces and the period are typed like letters. What the child has written fills a frame below
    it (up to three lines, the oldest scrolling away). A slip (a wrong letter, a letter where the
    space belongs, a space inside a word) erases the word in progress and the last written word,
    and the band rewinds to the start of the erased word; it never erases behind the last rewind
    until two words were written past it, so every child finishes. Sentences of 4-6 known words
    come until both minimums are met; erased words count once.
  - *Cyclic copy* (the placeholder of every level of a world not built yet, see
    [story](story.md)): the world's own background bands, the near one drifting slowly, Tapko
    at his usual place, and a small sign at the top with the world's name and "Coming soon".
    One target at a time on a large panel in the middle, above Tapko's head, soft white with
    the world's accent colour as its border and glow. Levels 1-2 a word, 3-4 a phrase of 2-3
    words, the super level a sentence of 3-5 words once space and period are known (else a
    phrase); the level's keys give the letters. A finished target floats up out of the panel
    and the next one grows in; the words typed are counted small under
    the panel. A slip only counts. Targets come without end until the minimum time is
    played; the target typed then is the last, and the end says how many words
    were typed. Its map dot is the copy envelope.
  - *Gust* (surprise of the meadow's level 3): half way through, the character says that
    the wind blows, and for about 25 seconds up to 3 small leaves (words of at most 3
    letters) come at once and fly slower. Once per run; a leaf flying by is a goal as
    always.
- **Ways to type** (a style per run, the same movement and story in every mode):
  - *Copy* (default, every story level): the child types the word shown.
  - *Dictation*: the voice says each target when it becomes the one to type; its label
    shows a speaker and one dot per letter (spaces as gaps), and the typed letters
    appear in place. A wrong key shows the whole target, which stays shown until done.
    A click or tap on the label says it again, and the voice repeats it once by itself
    after 4 seconds without a key. Targets get one and a half times the travel time.
    Without a voice for the language the run is copy typing and the get-ready card says
    so; the reading-aloud button is hidden in a dictation (the voice is the task). What a
    dictation may say is in `.specify/learning/words.md`.
  - *Memory*: a target shows for a moment (longer for long words, shorter at a higher
    difficulty, with a small ring that empties), then its letters turn into dots and
    typing starts. Keys pressed while it shows are not counted and the narrator asks to wait:
    the one exception to "a word seen can be typed", clearly marked by the ring. A wrong
    key shows it again for a short flash; the letters typed so far stay. Targets get
    1.8 times the travel time (calm modes wait anyway).
  - *Mixed* (kept for the fairy story's music tree, not built yet): dictation and memory targets in turns, each
    with its own icon. Without a voice the run is memory only.
  - The mark `↵` of a target waiting for Enter stays visible in all of them, and the voice
    never says it.
  - In own practice the child picks the way to type; each button has a tooltip and one line
    under them says what the chosen one asks: Copy "You see the word and type it.",
    Dictation "You hear the word without seeing it. Type what you hear.", Remember it "The
    word shows for a moment and hides. Type it from memory."
  - In all of them, the finger guide does not light the key of a hidden letter, the paws
    show the target and type it as usual, and the end screen adds how many targets the
    child typed without the target being shown again ("by ear" / "from memory" / "by
    ear and from memory"). The get-ready tip of such a run is about listening or
    remembering.
- Pause anytime (Esc or a visible button); the game also pauses when the tab is hidden.
  The pause is a small card at the top: the words and the guide's next key stay in sight.
  "Small break" with one stretch to try, the round X (Escape too) to go on, and Continue,
  "Slower" (one step lower difficulty, the narrator confirms; automatic growth may raise it
  again after accurate play; not before play), "Quick exercise" (that stretch with a 20 s
  countdown on the card, then the pause again, the level waiting where it was; "Longer
  exercise" there opens the map's warm-up, where going on typing continues the level and
  cancelling returns to the pause) and the end of the level. The title and the stretch center on
  the whole card, with room above and below the stretch. Help is the level's "?", not a button
  here.
- **The end card** is one fixed card for every end (a level, a practice, staying a while, the
  target range, the parrot, free typing, the stretch break and a finished task), so a child
  always finds the same things in the same place. It fits a small laptop screen (1024x600) and a
  phone without scrolling:
  - on top only the title of what ended and the round X;
  - in the middle on the left a square with one picture of what happened (the player's
    character after a level, the end's own picture elsewhere) and under it the stars (a level)
    or one short value (minutes, hits, words, exercises); on the right at most three rows
    "label - value" and at most one green line with the most important news; no narrator
    sentence, no chips, no exclamations besides the title;
  - when this run told a parent's surprise, a band with the open golden box and "Odomkol si
    nové prekvapenie!"; its button opens the surprises told today over the card;
  - at the bottom one orange main button first, at most two plain ones, and "Na mapu" always
    last on the right.
  The main button is, in order: "Prestávka" when a break is due (back from it the card goes on
  without it), "Pozrieť prekvapenie", "Odovzdať" when a task was finished in this run, then the
  end's own (the next level, the reward of a finished world, the next mission, play again). The
  green line takes the first that applies: a new or better world badge (with
  several rewards at once the first and "a N ďalšie odmeny"), new keys, a new medal, new things or
  a new character in the wardrobe, a smoother rhythm than last week; at the range a thing new in
  the collection, then the trained letter that improved most; with the parrot faster repeating
  than usual. A task still running shows as a row with its minutes left. Speeds are always
  letters per minute. The pause card and "Really end it?" are not ends and stay as they are.
- **Leaving a level** is said the same everywhere: "Stop typing" on the pause card of a story,
  an exercise or a hero's journey, so the child learns one button; every end card leaves with "Na mapu". Once something was typed in
  the level (a right or a wrong key) it asks first: "Really end it? This level will not
  count.", with "Stop typing" and "Continue", Continue focused. Ending keeps the time and keys typed in
  the statistics. Free typing keeps its X and leaves without asking, nothing is scored there.
- The **custom exercise** (untimed practice) has "Back" at the top left of the level bar, like
  every other screen: it ends the exercise and shows its results. Escape still pauses.
- **Staying a while**: the results card of a story level offers "Stay a while" after the main
  action, which keeps the focus. The same level, mode and background run again without an
  end: targets keep coming at the level's pace and Tapko says that Esc ends it; there is no
  progress bar. Esc pauses as anywhere, but the level is already done, so the pause card's
  first (focused) button is "Ďalšia úroveň" (when open), else "Ukončiť písanie": Enter after Esc
  goes on, the child need not look for the way out. Ending it shows
  the end card "Oddychové kolo hotové!" with the minutes, accuracy and rhythm, with Next
  level first (when open), A while more and the map. The run gives no stars, rewards, level result or unlocks; its keys and time count in
  the statistics and the lesson may advance, as after a practice. A super-obstacle has no
  endless form, so staying there plays the world's last regular level; the rocket's asteroids
  are the exception and keep coming, shielded pieces among them.
- **Peeking under the results**: on every results card (level, practice, staying a while,
  target range, parrot) Esc or the round X hides the card and its dimming; the last frame of
  the game stays in sight as it ended. Its top bar and the keyboard layout switch under the
  on-screen keyboard work as in play (help, voice, sound, the layout). A typed letter while
  peeking plays the same level again from its start without an end (staying a while; at the
  target range: staying there), where staying is offered. Esc, any other key, a click into the
  game or the pause button (nothing is left to pause) brings the card back as it was, with the same focus, and the key does nothing else;
  peeking never ends anything, so a child looking around loses no card.
- **Calm scenes breathe**: a scene that stands still (the calm steps of a world) lets one
  background band drift slowly on its own; with reduced motion nothing drifts. In a scene that
  moves with Tapko (its near band scrolls as he goes), the middle band never drifts: it moves
  only when he does.

## Why
- A results card that fills the screen hides the game and slows the next try; the child
  reads the stars and one or two facts, so those must be visible at a glance. Ends that each
  looked different and said many things at once confused children; one fixed card with one
  picture, a few rows and one piece of news reads at a glance, and the same buttons in the same
  place are learned once. A child also
  wants to see the scene they finished; peeking keeps the card one key away, and a second Esc
  never leaves a keyboard-only child stuck. A child pressing Esc to look around expects to come
  back, not to land on the map.
- A bar that filled on time alone and then waited for the last words, or waited for the time
  after the last word, looked broken; following the slower share makes it honest.
- The great swamp's log took six words and came only when the time left held them and the way
  to the shore; a slow child's level is barely 20 words, so the log almost never came and the
  story's promise went unseen. A fixed cost with a silent skip hides a moment; a cost scaled
  to the level and a test at both paces keep it in sight without stretching the level.
- The target range kept each ring where it was when the thing was thrown; hiding the keyboard
  widened the stage and the rings stood off the arc. A place kept in the scene's own terms
  cannot go stale.
- A frozen background in a calm level feels like the game stopped.
- The ribbon trains what word-by-word targets never do: keeping one's place in a running text
  and treating the space as one more key. Erasing the last word as well makes accuracy matter
  in a super level; a rewind that may not reach back further until two words are written keeps
  a struggling child moving forward. On the wind level a slip that only counted made the gust
  meaningless; restarting the word and a stronger, faster gust make the slip felt, and a few
  words more at most keep it small.
- Arcade-style typing (ZType, Typer Shark, Epistory) is praised as motivating; the peaceful
  variant keeps the fun without destruction or stress.
- Overlapping words cannot be read, and a child who cannot read the word cannot type it.
  A word seen but not yet typable feels like a broken key.
- The rhythm inside a word overstated how fast a child really is: quick bursts of known
  letters made the targets fly while reading took the time, and growth ignored the words
  that floated by. Speed pressure over accuracy is the top complaint about typing games
  for children; slow but correct must still succeed
  ([research](../../.research/features/accuracy-first-feedback.md)).
- Copying, hearing and remembering train different skills: dictation trains listening to
  letters, memory trains reading whole words instead of letter by letter
  ([research](../../.research/learning/exercise-types.md)). The style changes only how a
  target is shown, so every mode gets it without its own rules. A guide that lit the next
  key would turn both back into copying.
- Four levels of the same game in one session lose their novelty after the second;
  boredom is the mood that stays. Alternating action and calm steps (saw-tooth difficulty)
  and mixed practice keep children involved and help them keep the skill
  ([research](../../.research/game/world-rhythm.md)). A slip in the clean line costs a
  little more practice, never the row, so accuracy matters without fear.
- Some levels ended in under a minute, super levels with a few words in seconds: too short
  to settle into a rhythm, and stars came cheap.
- A pause that hides the game breaks the child's focus; a way to slow down without a
  parent keeps a struggling child playing instead of giving up.
- A child who likes a level wants to keep typing there, and replaying it again and again
  to collect stars would make stars cheap; staying a while can only improve accuracy and
  add play time.
- "Map" on the pause card did not say that it throws the level away, and it did so without
  asking. Every other screen has Back at the top left; the custom exercise was the odd one.
