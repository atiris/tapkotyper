# Parrot

## What
- **Papagáj** / **Parrot**: a voluntary side card in every world, next to the target range. The
  child types the same few words many times over, so the fingers learn them (muscle memory).
  Speed is not judged and there are no stars; slow typing is fine, the point is the practice.
- It opens with the world's super level, like the [target range](target-practice.md): a small
  parrot node after the range's target on the map. Its picture tells the parrot's state: its
  head while it is at home, else an empty short wooden perch (closed, not won yet, or flown
  away); its tooltip says what it trains. Before the world is finished the node is already there, grey and closed like a
  level not reached yet (the tooltip adds when it opens). It starts only when the child picks
  it, never by "Continue".
- **Scene:** the world's own background, only its middle strip drifting very slowly. Tapko
  stands at the left; at the right a wooden T-perch stands on the ground at the level of Tapko's
  feet, the parrot in front of it with its claws on the bar. The word to type is Tapko's bubble
  between them; the next word waits greyed after it. When a word is typed, the parrot repeats it
  in its own bubble with a short squawk, and every third word with a small hop.
- **Mistakes:** the third wrong key of a round sends the parrot up on a short flight round the
  scene (its wings beating, facing where it goes) with a parrot line ("Daj si kráátku
  prestáávku.", "Brrrzdi!", "Pozooórr.", "Opakovanie, matka múdrrosti.", "Trretií pokus"); it
  lands back and the round goes on. More wrong keys than 4 plus the story's number (5 in the
  dragon story, 6 in the robot's, 7 in the fairy's) and the parrot says "Veeéľa chýb!", "Skúús
  prresnejššie!" or "Prrriíliš neprresneé" and flies off: the word goes, nothing more is shown,
  and the round ends lost.
- **Round:** first the words in turn, three rounds (dom strom časy dom strom časy dom strom
  časy), then each word several times in a row; Tapko says when the second part starts. A wrong
  key brings the current word twice more, at most six more per word, so a round always ends.
- **Size** by the world's story and its place in it (worlds 1-2 of a story shorter words):

  | | Dragon story | Robot story | Fairy story |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Words | 3 | 4 | 5 |
  | Letters per word | 2-4, then 3-5 | 4-5, then 4-6 | 5-6, then 5-7 |
  | Repeats per word in part 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

  The words use the letters of the world's last level: real words where the letters allow it
  (letter drills in the meadow's tiny set), all different and starting with different letters
  where possible, new ones each round.
- The typing counts in the statistics and in the lesson like at the target range; paws, Enter
  marks and the parent's surprises stay out.
- **End:** a learned round ends with the parrot flying off the scene, and it is back on the end
  card: the parrot in colour, "Opakovanie s papagájom!", the words learned set apart in their own
  row, how fast the fingers were (the child's usual speed of the last 14 days, the learning part
  and the repeating part, the last one green when it was faster), new letters when the lesson
  moved on, and the Parrot badge with its line when this win brought it. "Nové slová" plays again
  with new words; the X at the top right and Esc show the game under the card, a second Esc goes
  to the map. Finishing the round wins the
  world's parrot.
- **Lost round:** no celebration; the end card shows the empty perch and says the parrot flew
  away and to try again slowly; "Hrať znova" is the main button. The world's parrot leaves home
  (the badge may go with it) and its map node shows the empty perch.
- **Parrots fly away**, so the child comes back and practises again:
  - one roll for each day since the last check (only today the first time): a 20 % chance that
    one random parrot at home flies away; a child back after 13 days gets 13 rolls;
  - never below the floor: the most parrots ever at home minus 3, and at least 1 (the last one
    stays);
  - 21 days after the last win the parrots drop straight to the floor.
  The roll runs when a player's map opens. The map then says it once in the narrator's bubble
  ("Uletel ti papagáj zo sveta Lúka. Choď poň!", several worlds in one line); the nodes already
  show their empty perches, no parrot flies from them.
- **Parrot badge** (Odznak Papagáj): held while the parrot of every finished world is at home
  (at least one). A newly finished world without its parrot takes it away until that parrot is
  won, and so does a parrot flying away. It is worth 7 points in the treasure chest
  ([statistics](../profiles/statistics.md)) and it is the one badge shown with the player: on the
  start page's player card as the parrot itself at the character's feet, by its left side; on
  the map and on "Môj postup" as **Aro**, the badge's parrot, sitting on a short wooden perch.
- **Aro** is the parrot's name; he says it when it fits ("Aro je dommaá!").
  - On "Môj postup" he sits under the character with a gap between them; a click makes him hop.
  - On the map (the character there a quarter larger than before) his perch stands left of the
    character, which moves a little right. A click: he says a takeoff line ("Zalieétamme si!",
    "Prrevetraáme krriídla!", "Štaárrtujem!", "Koóntrrola strraáánky!", "Inššpekčnyý let!",
    "Vzzlieétam!"), spreads his wings and flies off to the right across the page with beating
    wings, on one smooth curve that reaches down to its bottom part too, flies round and lands
    on the perch again. His lines stay long enough to read before he takes off, and he flies
    at a leisurely pace.
  - When the badge is lost (a parrot flew away or a round was lost) the next map shows him on the
    perch once more: after Tapko's line about the flown parrots he says goodbye ("Frrčiíím
    prreč!", "Odlieétam!", "Zdrrhaáám!", "Mizznemm!", "Guúúd baáj") and flies off to the right. The empty perch stays while that map
    is shown; the next map has no perch.
  - When the badge is won the first map after it shows the empty perch; Aro flies in from the
    right, lands and says hello ("Somm domma.", "Aro prrilieéta!", "Aro je dommaá!", "Dobrryý
    deň!", "Čaááuko.", "Ahhojj.", "Prristaávam!", "Maááš krrekrry?"); from then on he sits there.
  - With less motion he leaves and lands without the flight.

## Why
- Word streams and the range train reading and reacting; a few words typed many times train the
  movement itself, which is what makes typing blind.
- Parrots flying away bring the child back to old worlds for short practice, without any
  penalty: "a parrot flew away, go get it back". The floor and the last parrot keep it from
  feeling like losing everything.
- One badge next to the name gives a visible, regainable goal that rewards regular practice.
- The point is accuracy through repetition: a parrot flight after a few mistakes is a funny nudge
  to slow down, and a parrot that leaves after many makes careless rushing cost the round
  without any scolding.
