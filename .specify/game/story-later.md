# Later stories

The stories after the first, part of [the story](story.md); designed, built world by world.

## What

### Story 2: Bolt the Robot (*world 6 built, the rest not yet*)
A more technical story for children who like machines. **Bolt** (Šróbik in Slovak), a
small robot, is found and woken in the junkyard (world 6) and from the workshop (world 7) on
walks next to the child in every level of this story, a second figure that never
replaces the child's character: repaired after the junkyard, where he is found rusty and broken,
with new legs and a bright antenna after the workshop, in a yellow raincoat after the rainy town. He looks
worried at a mistake and happy at a finished word. In the junkyard he is not with the child:
he lies under the pile, and only the levels that find and wake him draw him.

6. **Junkyard** (designed with the owner 2026-11-15) - the child got the job of sorting a big
   junkyard, and from somewhere under the piles comes a sad beeping. Every level is a whole task
   of its own that ends in the level; the same junkyard background (rows of piles, a crane far
   back, springs and bolts in front) in all five.
   1. *Sorting the gears* (word stream): gears of four kinds roll in along the path toward a
      wooden crate with a gear sign, under a magnet on a crane cable. A typed gear hops up and
      rolls into the box; a missed one rolls past the character and away, and two more gears
      join the level (at most 10 words added), which the narrator explains the first time. A
      missed gear is more practice, never a failure. At the end the crate is full.
   2. *The pile of tires* (waiting words, calm step): a big pile of old tires blocks the path,
      a bin stands behind it. Two or three tires at its foot carry a word each; a typed tire
      rolls into the bin and a new one rolls down. The pile shrinks in three stages with the
      time played; at the end the way is free and the character walks on.
   3. *The magnet over the screws* (crossroads): a magnet on a crane cable hangs over a pile of
      screws and gears, on one of five places (the centre, one or two steps left or right).
      Arrow plates around it carry words; a typed plate moves the magnet a step sideways or
      lifts and lowers it. After each move it pulls: a big screw or a gear at the centre, a
      small screw one step off, nothing two steps off. The way back to the centre is the hard
      word, the way out the easy one, so the child chooses between easy and worth more; two
      steps off the level's clock does not run on until the magnet comes back, and the
      character says the harder signs pull more.
   4. *The beeping pile* (ribbon): a big pile of mixed junk beeps softly now and then; the
      text runs as the ribbon across the top. Each finished word pops a thing off the pile
      into one of three bins, each slip drops a thing back onto it. The pile shrinks in three
      stages with the level's progress; at the end Bolt lies where it stood, beeps once and
      does not move.
   5. *Bolt's program* (super level, the first transcribe): Bolt's program stands on an old
      green screen and the child copies it onto an empty screen of another width, so the lines
      break elsewhere and the child keeps track of where they are in the source. The source
      greys what is copied; a wrong letter is not written and adds one word to the program (at
      most 10), nothing is ever erased. Each finished line lights Bolt's eyes brighter; at the
      end he beeps, gets up and hops with the character. Reward: Bolt and the child are friends.
7. **Workshop** - parts (gears, bolts, wheels, springs, an antenna) ride on a conveyor
   belt; a typed part lands in Bolt's basket, the others ride on. Super-obstacle: Bolt's
   battery, charged bar by bar by a steady rhythm. Reward: new legs and antenna.
8. **Rain** (Dážď) - raindrops fall toward Bolt, who must not rust; a typed drop is caught
   under the child's umbrella, a missed one lands on Bolt, who shakes like a dog and giggles
   (no failure). Super-obstacle: a grumpy storm cloud calmed by rhythm. Reward: a raincoat
   for Bolt and an umbrella for the child.
9. **City** (Mesto) - locked doors with one to three password panels; typing every panel's
   password opens the door with a chime. Super-obstacle: the main gate, whose long code
   must be typed without a single mistake. Reward: Bolt's home key card.
10. **Home** (Domov) - dark rooms; each typed word switches on a lamp and wakes a helper
    robot that waves. Super-obstacle: Bolt's family welcome, little robots popping out of
    boxes (like the friendly mole). Reward: the family thanks the child, and the
    hero's journey opens.

### Story 3: Melody the Fairy (*not built yet*)
A gentle, musical story that is meant to appeal more to girls than the technical robot
story, while staying open to every child, and the story where listening and remembering
make sense: the forest has forgotten its songs and fell quiet, and each world brings one
song back. **Melody** (Melódia in Slovak), a small fairy with shimmering wings, floats
next to the child in every level, a second figure like Bolt that never replaces the child's character. She sings
the words she dictates, looks curious at a mistake and hops at a finished word, and has
a short line at the start of a level, after repeated mistakes and at its end.

11. **Garden** (Záhrada) - closed buds stand along the path; a typed word opens a bud,
    which hums one note (copy typing). Super-obstacle: the big sleeping flower, woken by
    six short dictated words, the first taste of listening. Reward: the garden's song and
    a flower wreath for the child.
12. **Path** (Cestička) - birds on branches sing their words (dictation); a typed word
    lets the bird fly along with the child, a missed one keeps singing in its tree. Super-
    obstacle: the owl's riddles, short sentences dictated word by word. Reward: a feather.
13. **Lake** (Jazero) - a word is reflected on the water, then a ripple washes it away
    (memory); a word typed from memory makes a lily bloom and a frog hop across.
    Super-obstacle: the long reflection, a whole sentence shown once and typed word by
    word from memory. Reward: a water lily.
14. **Tree** (Strom) - notes hang on the music tree; some sing (dictation), some glow and
    fade (memory), in turns; a typed note flies up and the tree plays. Super-obstacle:
    the tree's song, dictated short words to a calm steady beat. Reward: a little harp.
15. **Ball** (Ples) - fireflies drift down carrying short sentences of three to six words
    that Melody sings; each typed one lights the dance floor more. Super-obstacle: the
    last song, short sentences dictated one after another, and at the end the whole
    forest sings. Reward: the forest thanks the child and Melody gives them fairy wings, and the
    hero's journey opens.

The first level of the path and the lake starts with single short words. Without a voice
for the language the dictation worlds play as copy typing and the tree as memory, so the
story stays playable in any browser ([mechanics](mechanics.md)).
