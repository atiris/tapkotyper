# Story

## What
The child travels through worlds grouped into stories. Each world has several short
levels and ends with a super-obstacle and a reward that opens the next world. The story
is told on the get-ready card before each level, never in a scene of its own before play:
the first time the first level of a world is played, the card tells the world's story (what
happened, where the child is and what to type, the key phrases in bold); every other time the
level's own story line. Only the reward of a finished world is a short scene (a few lines of
text and a simple animation), opened from the results; the round X of its text panel, Skip
and Esc end it.
The child plays as their character ([the character](../companion/tapko.md)); a narrator
tells the story to the child in the second person ("you catch the leaf", "Melody
gives you a wreath"), never about the character as someone else.

### Worlds not built yet
Only the first four worlds (meadow, rocket, planet, valley) are built on the agreed rules. Until a
world is redone, all five of its levels, the super level too, play one simple placeholder
game, **cyclic copy** ([mechanics](mechanics.md)), so the child still progresses: playing a
level's round time unlocks the next one, and new letters keep coming as in any level. The
get-ready card says the level is still being made instead of its story (the world's story is
kept for when the world is built), and a small sign at the top says "Coming soon". World
names, map cards, badges, rewards, reward scenes, key needs and the story unlock rules stay
as they are. The world descriptions below marked *not built yet* are the design to build.

### Story 1: The Dragon Egg

1. **Training Meadow** - the meadow animals play with the child: the wind brings them leaves
   and they want to score a goal with them. A bee, butterfly, ladybug, fly or mouse brings a
   leaf with a word toward the child's character, who keeps goal with a pair of goalkeeper
   gloves held just beside it, toward the leaves, never covering the character. At the penalty area the creature kicks the leaf on;
   when the middle band above the character is free and the leaf flies elsewhere, the kick
   lifts it in an arc up there.
   The meadow world is one afternoon: training for the afternoon's football match (level 1), a
   bunch of poppies (2), the match itself (3), invitations to a treat after the match (4), and
   the hedgehog who missed the match and wants to play too, a good player, so it is harder (5). Typing the word sends the leaf faster into the gloves, which
   rise with it step by step and reach its height just as it arrives, so they always catch it: in the middle band when it was typed before the kick or kicked up there,
   else at its own height. The caught leaf fades away in the gloves, and its creature turns
   and goes home. The world's story and the level's start line tell exactly this, on the get-ready
   card of every story level under its title. A leaf not typed in time flies past: a goal, everybody cheers (a "GOAL!" pop,
   the creature's joy before it leaves, but no words from the narrator, which would nag), so a miss is
   a laugh, not a failure. The leaf size shows the word length at a glance. Animals are
   never the target of anything (no webs, nets or swatters): even soft hitting or catching
   is the wrong message for small children; playing with them is better.
   The meadow plays the world rhythm (see [mechanics](mechanics.md)): level 1 is the leaf
   game with single chunks; level 2 is the calm step, growing a bouquet of corn poppies: a bud
   grows out of the near grass with a letter above it (up to three at once, each with another
   first letter); the typed letter makes it bloom (half of the buds open slowly first), the
   flower stays a while, fades and drops its petals, and its seed head goes, while a new bud
   grows elsewhere; some poppies grow mirrored so they do not all look alike; every typed letter
   adds a poppy to the bouquet that gathers in the middle at the end, once the last poppy is gone,
   with praise for the bouquet ("Krásna kytica vlčích makov!"). The poppies stand about three
   quarters of Tapko's height. In the
   calm meadow levels the middle band of the background drifts slowly; level 3 is the leaf game again with a gust of
   wind half way; level 4 is the twist, the invitation to the meadow party written word by
   word without a slip, and every finished card flies as an envelope to a meadow friend. At
   the end one animal of each kind that got a card comes to the middle, under the line how many
   animals got an invitation (one per word typed right) and came for the treat after the match. On the meadow a
   leaf's word shows only once the animal carrying it is wholly in sight.
   Super-obstacle: a hedgehog shakes volleys of leaves off its back; they scatter around him,
   some forward, some behind him, and fly on toward Tapko from there, each word showing once
   its leaf has landed from the toss.
   Reward: a secret invitation to space. Before the first level the narrator
   shows the child their character: "This is you."
2. **Space Rocket Flight** - Tapko is sent to explore a few planets and reach the dragons' planet,
   avoiding every danger on the way: a long flight fast enough between the meteors (level 1),
   back to the rocket across the meteors after exploring the first planet (2), the flight to the
   second planet (3), calling the rocket with commands after exploring it (4), and the last part
   of the journey with the dragons' planet in sight (5). Tapko suits up where he stands: his things fade away, a white
   space suit grows up from the boots and a helmet with an opaque visor comes down over his
   head. The astronaut hops into the rocket, which from then on shows him in its round window.
   Typing rhythm drives the speedometer, the rocket's own instrument, which sits still near the
   bottom edge (half its height above it) under the rocket while the rocket bobs; a soft energy
   beam pushes asteroids and planets out of the path, some of them mirrored so they do not all
   look alike. The stream flies as one: all things in it keep one speed, the one its slowest
   word needs, so none overtakes another, and a new speed comes gradually over a few seconds.
   The space keeps the child's pace: a moment after the last key (longer than a pause between
   words) everything slows down gently over about two seconds (the things flying in take longer
   to arrive, the speedometer sinks with them) and speeds up just as gently with the next keys;
   no change jumps. The levels follow the world rhythm: 1 the stream of rocks; 2 the calm
   step, the astronaut hops from meteor to meteor (each with a short word) and the view glides
   after him; a new meteor appears at its place, fading in and growing, and its word shows once
   it is full size; meteors left behind stay where they are and leave the screen as the view
   glides on; every meteor is turned its own way (so are the things of the streams); he jumps
   onto the next meteor or over one, never over two: then he says "Jaj, tam nedoskočím." and
   the word stays on that meteor. The level counts jumps, whether over one meteor or none, and
   at the end the rocket, empty with clear glass in its window, comes for him from the left
   behind him, he jumps into its window and it flies on to the right; 3 a faster stream where
   about every fourth meteor is stronger: it glows, and when its word is typed it shouts ("Au!",
   "Zásah!", "Trefa!"), loses its glow and shakes in place, keeping the same word for a second
   hit and counting once (the opening line explains it); 4 the copy twist: the astronaut on a
   meteor writes commands to the rocket, which faces him on the right, on a command panel fixed
   in the middle above, with the onboard computer standing on its left edge (the meadow
   invitation's copy rules). A command with every word right is accepted, one with no word
   right rejected, one with both either way, half of the time each, so slips matter without
   ending the level. Accepted, the rocket fires its engine, comes closer by the level's fair
   share and back toward the middle line, and answers ("Inštrukcia prijatá.", "Rozumiem.",
   "Krátky zážih motorov."); rejected, it answers ("Príkaz neprijatý!", "Nerozumiem, skús
   znova.", "Signál sa stratil.") and veers up or down instead. The rocket moves only on a
   command, and the last command always brings it beside him. Its answer is above it, or below
   when it would cover the console. He jumps in, is glad, and the rocket flies away to the
   left; 5 the super-obstacle, whose big asteroids turn each their own way and whose last one
   flies at the rocket; two to four small pieces at random have a shield (a soft cyan bubble), and
   staying a while keeps bringing them: a shielded piece's
   word is typed twice, the first time the shield pops and the piece stays with the same word
   (the opening line says so). The near band drifts slowly on its own in the hop and command
   levels, where the scene stands still. The near
   rocks drift at half the usual near-layer speed.
   Super-obstacle: fractured asteroid.
   Reward: landing clearance.
3. **New Planet** (the dragons' planet) - its Crystal of Life lost its light, and Tapko brings
   it back. The planet looks almost like the Earth (green hills, a river), with giant mushroom
   trees, big pale crystals and small orange sawtooth clouds; one far background for the world
   and two sets of closer bands with little detail, so the game's things stand out: a swamp of
   calm blue-green water with mossy islands, reeds and a willow (levels 1 and 5), and a meadow of
   open grass with a few trees and mushroom trees and a sandy path in front with about five small
   crystals and mushrooms along its upper edge per screen (levels 2 to 4 and the side
   activities). On the meadow Tapko stands in the middle of the path in every level and side
   activity; the molehills stand on the path too. Tapko is as big as on the meadow; the word he types is well above his head, never
   over him or touching it.
   1 the swamp after the landing (waiting words, a calm minute): one word at a time above him,
   a ring on the water where the next stone comes; typed, the stone is there and he jumps onto
   it, the view glides after him. The stones are round mossy stones without a face, in three
   looks, each one picked at random but never the same as the stone before, nothing under them, and they lie at a little different heights; he lands two thirds up a
   stone. He moves toward the right edge as the level goes on. When about nine tenths of the
   level are done (or its words are, with fewer than ten seconds left), the shore comes in front
   of the swamp band, its earthen cliff just past the right edge (a separate picture of a grassy
   bank, so the band never needs a seam or mirrored copies; its far end never shows); the words go on stone by stone and the last one leads onto the grass, where he walks
   a little way in and says he can see the meadow and looks forward to it.
   2 the meadow's crystals (a stream): five molehills (fewer on a narrow screen), lumpy heaps
   with pebbles at their foot that sit anywhere low on the ground; a crystal of one of three kinds,
   turned a little its own way and glowing softly in its own color, grows slowly out from
   behind a heap (three kinds, each also mirrored) while its word sinks two thirds of the way
   down, stays big (about 30 % of Tapko's height) and cracks; a cracked one stays
   a moment longer before it fades. Its word shows high above it as it starts and sinks to
   its tip; typed in time, the crystal flies into Tapko's pocket (the rocket needs this energy
   for the way home); not typed, it cracks, its word goes, it fades and later a new one grows
   there. Only collected crystals count; the end says how many he has.
   3 crossroads: five places from left to right on the path, he and the posts in the middle of
   it; Tapko's words come one at a time above him
   and none can be skipped, each typed word a step on. At a crossroads his word goes and up to
   four signposts (three at the first one) grow almost together in the other places: posts of
   three heights, alternated so that no two within two places share one, each with its
   activation word on top. A slip inside a sign's word makes that
   sign vanish; the last one left must be typed without a slip (a slip starts its word again).
   Backspace wipes the started sign's word so another can be chosen; Backspace's control-key
   card before the level teaches it ([finger guide](../learning/finger-guide.md)).
   Each post carries a long wooden arrow board (two kinds) pinned by its middle, with the path's
   name written along it, as long as the name needs, turned around its middle its own way (never
   straight up or down, the name always upright). The paws never type a sign: at a crossroads
   they stop and the child chooses. The chosen sign's arrow spins and stops pointing up, the
   other signs go and that one vanishes last; then he walks to its place and the road goes on
   there. The paths: an ordinary path
   (cesta, chodník, alej...; ordinary words), a shortcut (a harder word; shorter words, a bit
   less time and fewer words), a fast path (harder still; short words, the next crossroads
   sooner, less time), a teleport (an extreme word; far fewer words and much less time), a
   detour (an easy word; more words and time), a hard overgrown path (a very easy word; harder
   words, more words and time), a wide easy road (a harder word; easier words, a few more of
   them, fewer crossroads), the dark forest (less time; some words echo, coming again up to
   twice in a row, and one is extreme), the unknown road (word length, road length and word
   difficulty each drawn at random, and Tapko says what happened) and the stony practice path
   (its words train the keys the child still practises, as the target range does).
   4 spells (copy twist): three crystals of life of three kinds, asleep (grey), peek out of a
   big molehill on the right, and now and then one of them gives a little shake. A spell of
   several words is written on a panel in the middle, word by word; a long one comes page by page
   (at most 14 words at once). Three slips break a page: three
   different small mushrooms grow from the top edge of the panel (high in the scene) and one goes at each slip; when the third
   goes, the page breaks and new words come in it. Each spell cast lights the next crystal with a shout
   ("Jupí!", "Zipp!", "Juchú!"); all three lit, the crystals fly to Tapko. The spells are long enough to fill the level's
   minimum time at the child's pace; the last one gets another page while the minimum is not played.
   5 the great swamp (super level): from a firm stone Tapko jumps three stones on, the fourth
   jump reaches the next firm stone and the view moves on. A slip on the firm stone is only a
   slip; a slip on any other stone cracks it (the stone shakes),
   he jumps back stone by stone (each one left crumbles, half broken, and the
   next one cracks) and on the firm stone a new word comes. About halfway (the bar at half) a
   little mossy log floats where a firm stone would be, bobbing gently on the water; Tapko says
   it would not hold him and he has to wait until it floats away. Six words push it away (a word
   with a slip pushes half), each a bit to the right, the sixth off the stage; a stone comes up
   where it lay and he jumps onto it. Never while the shore is near. Stones keep coming until
   both minimums (time and words) are nearly met, so a quick child never runs out of stones;
   only then the shore comes just past the stage's right edge, the last stone just before it.
   Every jump is a typed word up to the last stone; the shore's end never shows. From it he steps onto the grass, where a longer sentence of known words (a capital and a period once known) wakes the monolith: it must be typed without a
   slip, a slip brings a new one. Then the **monolith** (a carved stone pedestal, not an altar)
   rises out of the grass beside him, its foot a bit low with a soft dark green shadow behind it, a soft light
   above it; the crystals of life fly onto it, float in their glow and pulse, the planet blooms,
   and a few seconds later the level ends. The progress bar is full once the sentence is typed. Super-obstacle: the great swamp and the log. Reward: **the Force**, the crystals'
   thanks. The planet's own thing at the target range is a crystal.
4. **Dragon Valley** - the baby dragons' home is a big fire pit at the valley's entrance. Its
   fire went out when the crystal of life lost its light: the little flames scattered, the path
   into the valley grew over and the babies, cold and scared, hid deep in it. They never learned
   to fly, and their place is the crystal meadow (the planet's level 2). Tapko lights the fire,
   clears the path, teaches them to fly, sings them to sleep and in the morning helps them fly
   off. The babies are never targets: words sit on flames, bushes, a panel or a bubble high up.
   Tapko stands on the left as everywhere; in levels 3-5 the burning pit stands on the ground on
   the right (about 0.8 of the stage) and the dragons sit round it. There are four baby dragons,
   mint, sky blue, sunny yellow and red; their colours take turns (a fifth repeats the first), and
   each sleeps in its own colour.
   1 the flames (a word stream): little flames of four sizes (one look, a bigger one for a longer
   word, some mirrored) fly from the right in soft arcs; a flame's word shows once it is wholly in
   sight. Tapko holds an unlit torch upright just right of him, the dead fire pit a little further
   right. A typed flame speeds into the torch, which rises to meet it and always catches it; the
   first one lights the torch, whose small flame never grows. The torch then tips over in an arc
   and hands the flame to the fire. A burning pit is the pit with the four flames standing close
   and overlapping in it, the smallest in front and the biggest at the back, their feet at the
   middle of its inside, each flickering its own irregular way; sparks rise from it. The first
   caught flame lights it, and its fire grows slowly with the level's bar: until two thirds of
   the level one small flame at half height, then a second behind it, a third, and the biggest
   only with the last catches; all flames grow from half to full height, the full fire comes at
   the very end. Its warm glow and sparks grow with the level too. A flame not typed flies past the torch, goes out (a curl of smoke with a puff)
   and fades; nothing else happens. End: the fire burns and the dragons will see it from the
   valley. When the fire is blown out (the take-off's end) its flames shrink away from the back
   with a burst of sparks and smoke rises.

   The scene stands still, and a warm glow round the torch's top sets it off from it. A
   caught flame shrinks on its way and is gone in the torch, which sparkles, as the fire does
   when the torch hands it over.
   2 the overgrown path (a calm step, growing copy): bushes and branches (four kinds in random
   order, some mirrored) stand on the path one after another; the nearest has its spell, one
   word, above it, and the view glides as Tapko walks to it. Typed without a slip, the bush
   shrinks away. A slip turns the word red, it fades, two more bushes grow on the path ahead and
   the same spell shows again; bushes grow at most three times (6 words), after that a slip only
   shows the spell again. Once space is known, one big bush (several stacked) about two thirds
   along asks for two words with a space, as one spell; before that there is no big bush. The first slip, and then one in
   ten, says why ("Kúzlo sa nepodarilo.", "To bolo kúzlo na varenie čaju!"). The four dragons
   sit on the path from the start, facing Tapko and calm (only breathing), past the last bush
   and the room the slips may still grow, so a slip never moves them (the new bushes grow into
   the empty path before them, also on a wide, low screen); they come into sight with the path,
   never faded in. Only bushes the level's minimum adds beyond that room push them on, with a
   hop if they are in sight. End: the path is open, the dragons are glad (they
   hop and cheer) and Tapko walks on to them. Its map dot is the copy envelope.
   3 flying lessons (a stream with rhythm): a dragon (the colours take turns) waits right of
   Tapko, the three round leafy bushes stand together in the middle (random order, some
   mirrored), the burning pit stands at the right edge (about 0.95 of the stage, wholly on it)
   and the dragons sit left of it, out of its fire, each a little further left and higher,
   overlapping. Words come one at a time in a bubble high in
   the middle. The first typed word starts the flight, a slow arc to its seat (about 22 seconds)
   whose height follows the rhythm, full from 80 % of the rhythm goal; a pause in typing takes
   the lift away (from 1.2 seconds on, all of it in 2 more). The dragon leans nose up and flaps
   while it rises, leans nose down and glides while it sinks, and rocks gently. Too low over the
   bushes it lands in them, giggles ("Júj, to šteklí!"), climbs out and walks to its seat; past
   the bushes with the typing stopped it drops, lands hard ("Au! To bolo tvrdé pristátie.") and
   walks on (it still flew); high enough, it glides on and lands at its seat with a hop. A dragon
   is as big seated as flying; just before it touches down it turns upright into its sitting
   pose. Slips during one flight count for that dragon: the second makes it cry out ("Jaj!",
   "Pozor!") and wobble as it flies on; the third ends the flight at once - before or over the
   bushes it glides down into them (as too low), past them it drops and lands hard (it still
   flew). Words typed while it walks still count; the next dragon starts once it is there. End: how many of
   the dragons learned to fly; the stars count them too (all of them 3, at least half 2, else 1,
   never more than the accuracy gives). Its pit is smaller than the others and the seated
   dragons are glad now and then (a little hop).
   4 the lullaby (copy twist): evening; the dragons circle slowly above the burning pit, leaning
   into their flight. A panel top left, above Tapko's head and left of the circling dragons,
   shows a verse of the lullaby: two lines one under the other, each a sentence of four or five
   short words ending with a period (the period is new in this world). Only the line to type is
   a target; a typed one stays in ink, the one to come is faint. A verse copied puts one dragon
   to sleep: it glides down and sleeps by the fire in its own colour, no bigger than the flying
   ones, with a small "z z", the sleepers at different heights (a higher one is further back). A
   line with a slip fades red and is written again from its start (a typed first line stays
   typed); every third slip in the same line first makes it shorter (about its last quarter goes,
   at whole words, the period stays), so every child finishes. When the last circling dragon goes
   to sleep while the level still needs time, a new one flies in. End: all sleep by the fire.
   5 the take-off (super level): morning; the dragons sit by the pit facing left and one by one
   fly to the left. A flight is sets of 2-3 short words in a row above the middle, 3 sets for the first
   dragon and 4 for the others. Each word has its time from the moment it is the one to type
   (the child's own pace, tighter than a stream's), shown by a round clock right of the row in
   the look of the HUD's round buttons: a green ring that empties round an hourglass and blinks
   in its last quarter. Every try starts with the dragon sitting by the pit: its first set has
   no clock (a slip only starts that word again) and the dragon does not turn; typed, it lifts
   off to about the middle of the stage and the clock runs from then on. The flying dragon turns
   so its back is about level, sways gently round that and pulses a very little narrower (no
   bobbing up and down), follows the typing smoothly, letter by letter, quicker with a better rhythm, and
   sinks a little while the typing pauses. A set typed without a slip, every word in time, lifts the dragon a
   step up and on; a late word (it counts as a mistake) or a slip sends it gliding back to the pit ("Skúsime to znova!")
   and the flight starts again with new sets. After its third return a dragon's tries have one
   set less and 1.25 times the time; after the sixth its sets have two words and a slip or a late
   word only starts that word again, so every child finishes. After its last set the dragon flies
   off to the left. The paws are off. When the last is gone Tapko calls after them ("Ahojte!"),
   a quiet moment, then the fire flares up and an egg (without a face) is laid in it under the
   big flames; the fire is blown out with a burst of sparks and the egg sits in the dead pit with
   a soft glow. It greets Tapko ("Ahoj, ty budeš môj kamarát?"), and Tapko is glad and cheers.
   In the take-off a dragon that comes later flies in from the right in a high half-arc (in the
   upper half of the stage) and glides down a quarter-arc to its seat by the pit; the next try
   waits until it sits.
   Flying lessons, the lullaby and the take-off start with three dragons; when the last is done
   while the level's minimum time is not played, one more comes, at most five, and the fifth
   ends the level even below the minimum (played on without an end, one more always comes).
   Reward: the **Dragon Egg** that came from the dragons' fire. The valley's own thing at the
   target range is a flame (kept although the world got new things).
5. **Egg Journey** (*not built yet*) - the hatching egg rolls downhill; typing makes it jump over rocks and
   stumps. Super-obstacle: a friendly mole. Reward: a new companion hatches and
   **endless rescue missions** unlock (replayable, generated from all worlds).

### Story 2: Bolt the Robot (*not built yet*)
A more technical story for children who like machines. **Bolt** (Šróbik in Slovak), a
small robot, walks next to the child in every level of this story, a second figure that never
replaces the child's character: rusty and broken in the junkyard, repaired after it, with new legs and a
bright antenna after the workshop, in a yellow raincoat after the rainy town. He looks
worried at a mistake and happy at a finished word.

6. **Junkyard** - Bolt beeps sadly under a pile of old things. Typed junk (tires, crates,
   cans, springs) is lifted away by a soft magnet crane, untyped junk slides aside; the
   pile over Bolt shrinks through the world. Super-obstacle: a big tangled cable knot,
   untied piece by piece. Reward: Bolt climbs out, can only beep his thanks, and he and
   the child are friends.
7. **Workshop** - parts (gears, bolts, wheels, springs, an antenna) ride on a conveyor
   belt; a typed part lands in Bolt's basket, the others ride on. Super-obstacle: Bolt's
   battery, charged bar by bar by a steady rhythm. Reward: new legs and antenna.
8. **Rain** (Dážď) - raindrops fall toward Bolt, who must not rust; a typed drop is caught
   under the child's umbrella, a missed one lands on Bolt, who shakes like a dog and giggles
   (no failure). Super-obstacle: a grumpy storm cloud calmed by rhythm. Reward: a raincoat
   for Bolt and an umbrella for the child.
9. **City** (Mesto) - locked doors with one to three password panels; typing every panel's
   password opens the door with a chime. Super-obstacle: the main gate, whose long code
   must be typed without a single mistake. Reward: Bolt's home key card.
10. **Home** (Domov) - dark rooms; each typed word switches on a lamp and wakes a helper
    robot that waves. Super-obstacle: Bolt's family welcome, little robots popping out of
    boxes (like the friendly mole). Reward: the family thanks the child, and rescue missions
    also repair little robots.

### Story 3: Melody the Fairy (*not built yet*)
A gentle, musical story that is meant to appeal more to girls than the technical robot
story, while staying open to every child, and the story where listening and remembering
make sense: the forest has forgotten its songs and fell quiet, and each world brings one
song back. **Melody** (Melódia in Slovak), a small fairy with shimmering wings, floats
next to the child in every level, a second figure like Bolt that never replaces the child's character. She sings
the words she dictates, looks curious at a mistake and hops at a finished word, and has
a short line at the start of a level, after repeated mistakes and at its end.

11. **Garden** (Záhrada) - closed buds stand along the path; a typed word opens a bud,
    which hums one note (copy typing). Super-obstacle: the big sleeping flower, woken by
    six short dictated words, the first taste of listening. Reward: the garden's song and
    a flower wreath for the child.
12. **Path** (Cestička) - birds on branches sing their words (dictation); a typed word
    lets the bird fly along with the child, a missed one keeps singing in its tree. Super-
    obstacle: the owl's riddles, short sentences dictated word by word. Reward: a feather.
13. **Lake** (Jazero) - a word is reflected on the water, then a ripple washes it away
    (memory); a word typed from memory makes a lily bloom and a frog hop across.
    Super-obstacle: the long reflection, a whole sentence shown once and typed word by
    word from memory. Reward: a water lily.
14. **Tree** (Strom) - notes hang on the music tree; some sing (dictation), some glow and
    fade (memory), in turns; a typed note flies up and the tree plays. Super-obstacle:
    the tree's song, dictated short words to a calm steady beat. Reward: a little harp.
15. **Ball** (Ples) - fireflies drift down carrying short sentences of three to six words
    that Melody sings; each typed one lights the dance floor more. Super-obstacle: the
    last song, short sentences dictated one after another, and at the end the whole
    forest sings. Reward: the forest thanks the child and Melody gives them fairy wings; rescue
    missions also guide little forest friends (baby birds and fireflies) home.

The first level of the path and the lake starts with single short words. Without a voice
for the language the dictation worlds play as copy typing and the tree as memory, so the
story stays playable in any browser ([mechanics](mechanics.md)).

### All stories
- Story 2 opens when the child knows 10 keys, story 3 at 20; from then on the stories go
  on independently. Lesson keys do not depend on stories, and later stories' levels need
  more keys ([lesson progression](../learning/lesson-progression.md)), since their words
  are harder; listening and remembering need richer words, so story 3 comes later than
  the robot story.
- Worlds of a story unlock in order; finished levels can always be replayed. A level also
  needs a few known keys ([lesson progression](../learning/lesson-progression.md)).
- On the map each story is one row with its name above its world cards, left aligned with
  the first card; a closed story shows when it opens. World numbers run on across stories.
- Rescue missions open with the first finished story and play only level kinds of built
  worlds, never the placeholder. The friend met in a mission follows its story: a dragon, a
  little robot or a little forest friend (only dragons while stories 2 and 3 are not built).
  They collect on the **Little friends** screen, one group per story.
- Rescue missions happen at the **rescue camp**: an evening place with warm lanterns, a
  rope bridge and a lookout flag, so a mission looks different from replaying a world. The
  play keeps the mission's level kind and its friend. The paw never starts by itself in a
  mission unless the child turned the automatic paw on.
- Every level can be finished by any child: obstacles that are not cleared in time just
  pass by or are helped by Tapko; they never cause failure.
- World names are short words a young child reads at once, at most 12 characters (Lúka,
  Raketa, Planéta, Údolie, Vajíčko, Dážď, Mesto, Domov, Jazero); the scenes use the longer
  names (the Lily Lake).

### Cheers
- When a creature or Tapko is glad, a short cheer pops up over the scene with a cheer
  sound: "Hurá!", "Jupí!" or "Super!" ("Hooray!", "Yippee!", "Great!"). It comes when a
  super-obstacle is beaten (the hedgehog shouts it while it hops, Tapko when the world is
  done, also after the valley's take-off), when the owl's riddle is solved, a lake phrase is sung and the city gate opens.
- Once per such moment, never on every target: a cheer on every word would turn into
  noise and lose its joy.

### World badges
- Every finished world gives a badge to collect, a round medal with the world's picture:
  meadow an invitation envelope, space a rocket, planet a glowing hand (the Force),
  dragons a dragon egg, egg journey the hatched baby dragon, junkyard Bolt waving,
  workshop a gear with a spring leg, rain an umbrella, city a key card, home a house with
  a heart, garden a flower, path a feather, lake a lily, tree a note, ball a moon. The
  story reward scenes stay; the badge comes in addition.
- Its tier follows the world's stars (5 levels x 3): bronze when the world is done, silver
  at 12 stars, gold at 15 (three stars everywhere). The tier is the colour of its ring.
- The end screen says "New badge!" or "Your badge is silver / gold now!" when a level
  earns or improves one. The badge sits in the corner of its world card on the map, and
  the progress view has a badge shelf with a place for every world: earned ones in colour with
  their tier, the others as grey outlines that say which world to finish.
- A badge's tooltip shows the way to the next tier: bronze names what silver needs, silver
  what gold needs, each with the stars the child has in that world.

## Why
- The story gives a reason to practice without competition; replayable rescue missions
  give long-term variety without streak pressure.
- A rescue is a special event and should feel like one; a paw that types by itself takes
  the mission away from the child.
- The planet's levels were the same swamp twice with signs and a boulder in a mint and purple
  world; Tapko was smaller than in the worlds before and his word covered him. One story per
  level, the rules of the first two worlds and paths that change the level make each level
  its own; a slip that sends him back stone by stone makes the super level's accuracy visible.
  The stony practice path was added to the owner's paths: no other path trains the keys the
  child still needs. The whole story of the first three worlds is in
  [the scenario](story-1-scenario.md).
- Anything on the ground that stands higher on the screen is further back and is drawn behind
  what stands lower (the sleeping dragons, the seated ones, the planet's swamp stones).
- The valley was four times the rocket's game (space rocks falling on baby dragons, a shield
  bubble) and did not play the world rhythm. Nothing falls on anyone now: the targets are flames
  and bushes, and a dragon that does not make it giggles in the bushes and walks on. Each level
  has its reason (the fire is the dragons' home, the cleared path is where non-flyers walk, the
  flight learnt in level 3 is tested in level 5), the period, new here, makes the twist the first
  sentence copy, and the take-off asks for clean sets in the child's own pace but leaves nobody
  behind. The flight levels count dragons, not words; the arc is slow enough that five flights
  take about the minimum, so a rare fast typist may end one a little early.
- The old meadow text (goals as the happy outcome, a fan) described something else than the
  play and confused children. Goalkeeper gloves fit the animals' football game; a leaf kicked
  up to them is caught where the child looks, above their character.
- Tapko drawn small inside the rocket's window flickered; one picture of the suited pilot
  stays steady and looks the same for every companion. A speedometer bobbing with the rocket
  was hard to read.
- Things kept flying at full speed while the child stopped to think, and the speedometer
  sank only slowly; the time limit has to stretch the moment the typing stops.
- More stories keep older children playing; typing passwords at locked doors fits a
  typing game, and machines appeal to children whom dragons do not.
- A gentle, musical story speaks to children whom machines do not, and a forest that has
  forgotten its songs gives listening (dictation) and remembering (memory) a reason.
- Badges are things to collect, like the dragon egg, and a badge that grows with stars
  gives replaying a finished world a goal.
