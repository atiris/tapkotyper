# Super-obstacles

## What
Each world ends with a bigger, special challenge:
- **Hedgehog** (meadow): a hedgehog shakes volleys of two or three leaves at once off its
  back toward Tapko, each with its own word, a bit slower than in normal levels; the next
  volley comes when every leaf of the last one is saved or scored. Saved leaves fly back
  onto its back, goals are cheered as in the meadow. Several words at once train choosing
  a target without any failure. At the end it hops happily on the spot and shouts a cheer.
- **Fractured asteroid** (space): a big asteroid, when pushed, splits into smaller
  targets with shorter words. The big ones turn, each its own way (some mirrored), and the
  last one, bigger, flies straight at the rocket.
- **Cracking boulder / rock labyrinth** (planet): precise typing cracks it into safe
  stepping stones; accuracy matters more than speed here.
- **Rolling energy wave** (dragons): countered by a rhythm sequence - short letter groups
  typed to a steady beat. Off-beat typing still counts, only less smoothly.
- **Friendly mole** (egg journey): pops up unexpectedly with a short word that rewards a
  quick reaction; missing it just makes the mole wave and hide.
- **Cable knot** (junkyard): every short word unties one loop; an exact word unties a
  whole loop, one with mistakes half of it.
- **Battery** (workshop) and **storm cloud** (rainy town): rhythm sequences like the
  energy wave; the battery fills four bars, the cloud shrinks and the sun comes out.
- **Main gate** (city): a test of accuracy. The code is eight groups of six characters
  from the child's own keys, letters with any known punctuation and digits (every second
  group has at least one of them). The whole code shows at once in two rows of panels;
  the current group is typed in its panel and each typed group lights one part of the
  gate's ring. A wrong key starts the code again: the lit groups go dark with a soft
  buzz and the same code starts from its first group. A card says so before the first
  group, and after three restarts the narrator says once to go slowly and exactly, since no clock
  runs. There is no timer and no other loss, the paws are off (they would type the
  code for the child), and the stars come from the accuracy as usual.
- **Family welcome** (robot home): little robots pop out of boxes with short words, like
  the friendly mole.
- **Sleeping flower** (garden): six short dictated words, one at a time; each wakes the
  flower a little, one with a slip or a reveal half as much.
- **Owl's riddles** (path): short sentences dictated word by word; the words typed so far
  stand in the owl's speech bubble, so the riddle grows.
- **Long reflection** (lake): a whole sentence shows once on the water, then hides and is
  typed word by word from memory; a slip shows only that word again.
- **Tree's song** (tree): a rhythm sequence like the energy wave, with dictated words and
  a calmer beat, since hearing a word takes longer.
- **Last song** (ball): short dictated sentences one after another; at the end the whole
  forest sings.
- None of them can fail the level; the main gate can only start again.

## Why
- Variety keeps children engaged; low replay value is a top complaint about Dance Mat.
- The last gate of the robot story asks for exact typing, the skill the story trains,
  and stays fair for every child because nothing hurries or ends the try.
- The fairy story's super-obstacles grow listening and remembering from single words to
  whole sentences, the skills the story trains.
