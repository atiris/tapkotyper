# Target range

## What
- A fun, voluntary place to train exactly the letters that hold the next ones back:
  **Strelnica** / **Target range**. One for every world, on the world's own background and
  music; a world's range opens when its super level is done, and a small target node next to the
  super level's star on the map starts it; its tooltip says it trains the letters that lack
  accuracy, the way to the next letter when only accuracy is missing. Before that the node is
  already there, grey and closed like a level not reached yet, its tooltip adding that it opens
  when the world is finished.
- The letters trained are the keys of the newest lesson group that are not ready yet
  ([lesson progression](../learning/lesson-progression.md)); when none holds the lesson back,
  the three known letters with the lowest recent accuracy.
- The scene is the world's background with only its middle strip drifting very slowly; nothing
  casts a shadow. Tapko stands at the far left, his feet level with the target's legs.
- Tapko stands at the left and throws something funny at a target at the right, picked at
  random for each throw (never the same twice in a row) from about 35 things open in every
  world (a pebble, a tomato, an egg, an alarm clock, a cactus, a boomerang, a planet, a computer mouse, ...),
  three more in the robot story's worlds (a battery, a magnet, a gear) and three more in the
  fairy story's (a music note, a frog, a magic wand). The compass, the same picture as the node of the
  [hero's journey](heros-journey.md), is one of the things of every world: it bounces with a
  wobble and a sparkle, and rings. The child does not know what flies until
  it is thrown. A short word over Tapko's paw starts each throw, with no hurry; the rules said at the start
  stay up until the first throw, so a child has time to read them.
  It flies slowly and steadily through 4 soap-bubble rings toward the target; each ring and
  the target carry a word rich in the trained letters (5 words a throw), never of those
  letters alone while another word is there. The words grow from the shortest allowed length
  to the longest (one letter longer in the second half when both are the same). Each word gets
  the time a word of its length needs at the child's pace; typing it in time lets the thing
  pass the ring. The thing heads for the next ring; only in the second half of the way, while
  its word still waits, it drifts up a little, back again as the word gets typed. A ring passed
  too late pops grey and leaves it a bit higher; the throw goes on and still hits, only lower,
  like after a wrong key, so a word left out costs little. Only the target's own word typed too
  late sends the thing over it ("Missed!"). The middle needs every word in time and no wrong
  key. Words not typed are no mistakes: accuracy follows only the keys pressed.
- A wrong key makes it sink a little (a lower path). After three wrong keys in one throw it
  falls to the ground with a soft "plop" and lies there a moment, fading; nothing bad
  happens and the next throw comes.
- Passing every ring hits the target with a funny effect of the thing: the tomato splats, the
  fly sticks and buzzes, the alarm clock shakes and rings, the boomerang flies back into
  Tapko's paw, the butterfly flutters away, the ice cube slides down and melts, the light bulb
  lights up, a planet makes the target wobble under circling stars; the rest bounce off. An egg
  ends one of three ways: it splats, it hatches on the target (only in the middle), or it
  hatches in flight and the chick flies on.
- The range talks. The moment the launch word throws, Tapko names the thing in a small bubble
  ("Mucha vpred!"). About every other hit makes a word pop out of the target, above or left of it at a random angle: the thing's impact sound ("Pľac!") or a general one ("Bum!", "Kaboom!", "Auuuu!"). At every hit the target
  says how good the throw was in a bubble left of it, below the flight path: in the middle (no
  wrong key) mostly a funny line of the thing ("100-percentný kečup!"), one time in five a
  bullseye line; one mistake a near miss, two or more a decent hit; when a line names the points
  they are the hit's real points. A hit's points are the share of the letters of the words given
  in its flight, in percent, after a penalty that grows with the mistakes (wrong keys and late
  words): n mistakes cost n x (n + 1) letters, so 21 letters give 100, with one mistake 19/21
  (90), with two 15/21 (71); a miss or a fall gives 0. A miss
  and a fall get only a kind line of the target ("Vedľa!" among them, no word pops up); at the third and fifth bullseye in a row a streak line follows the hit line, so the thing's joke is not lost. The last throw
  of the base time and a target that steps back (shrinks) a line of their own. Now and then
  (about one throw in four, more for a cactus or a spider) the target reacts while the thing is
  on its way: worried, wondering or cheering. No line comes twice in a row. Every line stays long
  enough for a slow reader (longer lines longer); the next throw waits for the target's line a
  little, not until it is gone. The bubbles never cover a word; screen readers hear them, the
  voice never reads them.
- The things come in shuffled rounds: every thing of the range once before any comes again, a
  new order every run, never the same twice in a row.
- Now and then two big cartoon eyes peek out over the target's rim. Coming with a line before
  the hit (at the throw or a ring), they stay for the whole flight: they follow the thing,
  blink, go wide as it comes close, and react to the end (squeezed shut and then smiling at a
  bullseye, wide at a lower hit, dizzy after a planet or a bowling ball, glad after a miss or a
  fall) before they sink a moment later. Never two throws in a row; most throws the target has
  no face.
- Every world has one thing of its own that no other range throws (a ladybug on the meadow, a
  toy rocket in the rocket world, a fish at the lake, ...). It comes once a run, as the last
  throw of the base time or the one before (judged by the time left), so it is a goal to
  reach; while staying a while it comes every 6 to 10 throws. It never comes before the fourth
  throw, and only while every throw before went without a wrong key or a late word: after one
  careless throw the run goes without it. Hitting it in the middle (no
  wrong key, every word in time) the first time makes it a find, and Tapko says it is yours if
  you throw to the end: it joins the collection only when the run ends, not when it is left
  early. The end card shows all 15, found ones in color, the rest grey with a question mark
  whose tooltip names the world where it waits; the progress overview counts them too.
- With something in the collection, Enter swaps the thing in Tapko's paw (before its throw) for
  a random one of the collection, and Tapko names it; the rules at the start say so. The
  world's own thing still to be found cannot be swapped away.
- The target's size shows the recent accuracy on the trained letters: big at low accuracy,
  smaller as it improves. When a trained letter becomes ready during the run, the target
  shrinks with a little bounce, Tapko cheers, the narrator says so, and the letter leaves the
  list. The trained letters are said at the start, not shown in the HUD.
- At least two and a half minutes (the base time), counted from the first throw (reading the rules takes none of them): the
  run ends with the throw in the air after them, the same in every world (the range is
  for time only, never a number of throws). The HUD bar shows only the time; the hits and the
  points so far stand in the top right corner over the target ("Zásahy: 1   Body: 89") in the
  shouts' rounded bold letters, in their own colour. The last word, over the target, stands high
  enough that the target's eyes peeking out over its top stay in sight. The most points of a run
  are the range's record (kept per player with the day it was set, staying a while does not count;
  the progress's third row, the totals since the start, shows it and its day); the first time a
  run beats it Tapko says "Nový svetový rekord!". The end card (the one card of every end, see
  [mechanics](mechanics.md)) is "Strelnica hotová!" with the target, or "Nový svetový rekord!"
  with the winner's cup when the run set the record; under the picture all hits of the run, the
  rows the points, accuracy and the hits in the middle. Its green line tells a thing new in the
  collection (its picture and "Do zbierky ti pribudol nový predmet."), else new letters when the
  lesson advanced, else the trained letter whose accuracy grew most. Buttons: "Hrať znova",
  "Ešte chvíľu" and "Na mapu". No stars, no paw help, no Enter marks, no story friends; the
  parent's surprises counting training time are asked and told like after a practice (see
  [parent controls](../profiles/parent-controls.md)), never while staying. Typing while peeking
  under the card or "Ešte chvíľu" keeps throwing without an end (every hit counts); Esc there
  pauses with "Ukončiť písanie" first, and ending brings the card back.
  Good play counts toward growth like a practice.

## Why
- A child with low accuracy on a few letters is stuck and does not see why: new letters
  open only when the newest ones are accurate and steady. The map now names those letters
  ([lesson progression](../learning/lesson-progression.md)); the range is the way out.
- Plain drills of weak letters are boring; a throw that sinks on a mistake and splats on a
  hit makes accuracy, not speed, the game. Falling softly keeps mistakes harmless.
- A shrinking target makes the progress visible without numbers.
- A talking target and a surprise in every throw make a clean throw feel like a bullseye and
  keep the range funny for long; points and lines tell a child how good a throw was without a
  score to chase. The world things make the range worth playing in every world.
- A steady flight and growing words add a gentle time pressure, and the bullseye rewards a
  whole clean throw, so the range stays a game and not a drill.
