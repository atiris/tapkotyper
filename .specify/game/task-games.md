# Task games

How a teacher's task plays (its making and hand-in: [teacher links](../profiles/teacher-links.md)).

## What
- A task is one level of its own, outside the story: no map place, no stars, no level progress;
  only the task's run is kept (its best run counts). The lesson path may still advance after it,
  as after a practice. The parent's surprises, the story friends and the paw help stay out of it.
- **Before play:** the new-key cards the levels would show, then the get-ready card titled with
  the task's name: "Po stlačení medzerníka sa ti spustí úloha na písanie, ktorú si dostal. Píš
  pozorne a snaž sa písať čo najpresnejšie. Rýchlosť nie je taká dôležitá ako presnosť. Ak sa ti
  cvičenie nepodarí podľa tvojich predstáv, môžeš to potom skúsiť znova. Veľa šťastia pri písaní."
- **The scene:** Tapko stands on the left and does not move; the world's background is drawn as
  in its levels, without its obstacles or other things. The world is the one of the child's next
  level, or for a random task any world of the game, a locked one too (only as a backdrop; nothing
  opens).
- **The text:** a words game plays the task's words in order, then again shuffled; a text game
  plays its text sentence by sentence (split after `.`, `!` and `?`; a sentence longer than a line
  goes on at a word boundary). Characters the keyboard cannot type are left out.
- **Only known letters** (on): words with a letter the child has not learned (a capital needs its
  Shift too, a text needs the space) are left out, marks the child does not know are dropped. With
  nothing left the task plays dictionary words the child can type, as many as it had words or
  sentences; without the dictionary a card says the task needs letters still to learn, with one
  button back. **Off:** before play every letter of the task the child has not learned gets its
  new-key card, one after another, and the task uses them; they do not become learned keys.
- **Dictionary** (on): once the task's own words or text are given out and the limit is not
  reached, dictionary words the child can type come (sentences of them in a text game). Off: the
  task's own words or text come again.
- **Limit:** *Čas* - the task runs for its minutes of active typing (the levels' clock, which
  stops while the child waits over a word) and ends then, wherever the text is. *Text* - it ends
  when the task's own words or text are written once; nothing else comes. *Čas aj text* - both:
  done early, the typing goes on until the time is up; the time up first, it goes on until the
  text is written. The bar shows the limit still open. A word let pass comes again later, so the
  text is written whole.
- **Slips never send back:** a wrong key counts and the same place waits for the right key; in the
  copy games the place turns red a moment.
- **The end:** the end card "Úloha splnená!" with accuracy, rhythm, the minutes and whether this
  run became the best; **Odovzdať** (the best run) and **Hrať znova**. A task is played in one
  sitting: leaving early keeps nothing. Leaving goes back to the Tasks page.

## The five games
- **Prúd slov:** words fly in from the right edge toward Tapko one after another, at the pace and
  spacing of the levels' stream for this child; nothing but the words. A typed word bursts; a word
  reaching Tapko just passes, without harm.
- **Čakajúce slová:** three to five words (more for a quicker child) wait at free places in the
  right two thirds of the stage; the child types them in any order, the first letter picks the
  word. A typed word fades and a new one comes to a free place.
- **Odpis:** the panel of the valley's lullaby on the right, about 60 % of the stage wide. It
  shows as many lines as fit, each filled with sentences up to its width, never a broken word.
  The line to copy is lit, its next letter marked; it is typed letter by letter into the row under
  it; then the next line lights up, and after the last a page of the next sentences comes.
- **Pás:** the ribbon of the egg's spell in the right half, the text as one endless ribbon, spaces
  and periods too, with the frame of what is written under it; no hearth, egg or fire.
- **Prepis:** two panels side by side right of Tapko: the text to copy on the narrower left one,
  its next word softly lit, and what is written on the wider right one, wrapping at its own width
  with a cursor. The same words fall on other lines, so the child copies by reading, not by
  matching lines. The left page turns when it is copied.

## Why
- The teacher chooses exactly what the children practise and for how long; the child gets a calm
  scene with nothing to lose, where accuracy is what counts, and may try again for a better result.
- Known letters keep a task fair for a child early on the lesson path; teaching the missing
  letters first lets a teacher give any text when that matters more.
- Copying from a panel or from lines that wrap differently trains reading ahead, as when copying
  a text from paper.
