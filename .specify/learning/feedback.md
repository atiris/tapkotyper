# Feedback

## What
- Correct key: instant soft visual and sound confirmation.
- Wrong key: the target letter gently wobbles, the expected key glows on the keyboard,
  and the narrator may give a short hint. Nothing is lost; the child simply types the right key.
- After three mistakes on one key, the narrator shows which key it is and which finger presses it;
  play goes on, with no extra practice in between.
- Results show accuracy first (stars for clean words), speed only as a personal trend
  ("faster than last week"), never compared with other children.
- A parent switch "no time pressure" turns every timed element into an untimed one.
- Speed counts only real moves between keys: pressing the same key again
  ("ffff") adds nothing to the speed ring, the session's characters per minute or the
  day's reaction times. The ring opens below its top, follows the real pace and sinks
  within a few seconds once typing slows down or stops.
- **Three measures, in the owner's order of goals:** every level kind (story levels, the parrot,
  the target range, Rytmus, own practice, free typing, tasks) shows the same three rings in its top
  bar, after the progress bar: **Presnosť** (the level's share of right keys, a check at 100 %),
  **Rytmus** (how even the typing is, 0-100) and **Rýchlosť** (letters a minute). Each ring's
  tooltip names the measure and its value ("Presnosť: 96 %", "Rytmus: 82 zo 100 – ako rovnomerne
  píšeš", "Rýchlosť: 64 písmen za minútu"). The accuracy ring uses a log scale of the errors, so 80
  to 100 % fill most of it: red below 80 %, orange below 90 %, yellow above. Which rings show is a
  per-profile setting ([parent controls](../profiles/parent-controls.md)); by default only Presnosť.
- **Rhythm is evenness, not speed** ([research](../../.research/learning/rhythm-measure.md)): each
  time between two correct key strokes is compared with the one before it, so a steady slow child
  and a steady fast child score alike, a slow drift over the level costs nothing, and bursts and long
  hunts for a key show. Not measured: reading a new target (its first letter), the time across a
  pause or a card, the time around a wrong key or a Backspace, the same key again,
  and the time inside a combo (a dead key with its letter, Shift with a capital: one stroke from the
  first press). The natural difference between key pairs (two hands, one hand, one finger) does not
  count as unevenness. One long hunt makes one bounded mark. The ring shows "…" until about ten pairs
  of strokes are measured, eases toward new values and never sinks while the child is not typing.
  Rhythm never shows better than accuracy allows (no cap from 95 %, at most 50 at 85 %, 0 at 75 %).
  Rhythm never changes stars and is not on the end card; it goes into the statistics. Rytmus (the
  game) shows its own beat accuracy, beat score and tempo in the same three rings.

## Why
- Typing one key over and over in the middle of a story makes no sense to a child; the
  hint with the finger is enough, and own practice is there for drills.
- Holding or hammering one key faked speed: the ring stayed high and parents read wrong
  speeds in the statistics.
- A child who types in bursts used to get the best "rhythm", because the old gauge measured speed; the
  owner's order is accuracy, then rhythm, then speed, so the three are kept apart and named for what
  they measure. One set of rings everywhere means one thing to learn; a young child sees only
  accuracy by default, so the top bar stays calm.
- The per-key accuracy rings of the target range and own practice are gone: the key statistics keep
  that information, and the top bar shows the same measures in every level.
- Accuracy between 80 and 100 % decides whether a key is ready; a plain ring would show
  85 % and 98 % almost alike, so the child would not see the small gains that open the next letter.
- Racing and rigid scoring push speed over accuracy and discourage children
  ([research](../../.research/features/accuracy-first-feedback.md)).
