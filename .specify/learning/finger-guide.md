# Finger guide

## What
- On-screen keyboard (matching the profile layout) with a color per finger and a
  highlight on the next key; for dead-key letters both steps are shown. The letter on a
  highlighted key is dark or white, whichever reads better on that finger's color, the color darkened a little when needed
  to reach AA contrast.
- **Hands on the keyboard**: two outline hands seen from above lie on the on-screen keyboard
  in the home position, only their lines drawn (nothing inside them), so every key shows through;
  a see-through nail in each finger's color; the wrists fade out below the keyboard. For the
  next key the hand that types it takes that key's pose (the other stays home; for a capital it
  takes its Shift; for the space bar the thumb of the hand that did not type the last key goes
  down); each pose is the outline of a real hand seen from above typing that key, posed by the owner
  on a 3D hand, and one pose fades into the next. The hands lie slanted, as with the elbows wide. The key's letter is drawn over the hands (white, a thin outline in the
  finger's color, a small disc of that color fading from its middle), so a finger never hides
  it. On the get-ready card that opens a
  level the hands wait at the page's sides and slide onto the home position about a second
  later, or at once when the card closes; they then stay the whole level. They fade and hide
  with the guide, and are not in the heat map, the key pickers or the placement check.
- The guide fades as the child improves (per-key mastery) and can be shown or hidden by
  the child, or locked by the parent. The finger colors are a colorblind-safe palette; the keys
  carry no finger numbers.
- The keyboard has a real keyboard's shape: every row is 15 key units wide, with Tab, Caps Lock
  and Backspace drawn (they type nothing here), an L-shaped Enter on Slovak keyboards and a wide
  one on US ones, the Shifts and the space bar in their real widths and places. It keeps its
  proportions at every window size: it scales as a whole with the room it has, and a low window
  makes it narrower, never its keys flatter.
- "The adventure goes on!" card before every adventure (story level, hero's journey, own practice)
  and at breaks, the only card before a level apart from the key cards. Two panels side by side
  with a gap (one above the other on a narrow screen), no frames inside them: on the left a
  picture of good posture, a boy's or a girl's at random, with **one** line under it - the
  session's preparation tip when one is due, else one random reminder (sit straight, feet on
  the floor, wrists relaxed, index fingers on the `F` and `J` bumps, eyes on the screen); a
  stretching tip says it is for a short break. On the right the title on the line of the close
  button, under it the story in a larger paragraph centered in the room left, with its key phrases highlighted (the world's story the first time in a
  world, else the level's: what happens and what to type, with what the level asks in
  particular, such as the main gate's whole code without a mistake), no instructions in it;
  the story is not said again in a bubble when play starts. At the bottom, however short the
  story, the space bar, a drawn key. Under the picture, below its line, two lines "Fingers on
  the home position ASDF JKLÔ!" (the keys of the child's layout) and "Do not look at the
  keyboard, and press the space bar." show only when the space bar has not been pressed for
  10 seconds, so a child who reads the story is not told twice; they keep their room meanwhile,
  so nothing jumps. "home position ASDF JKLÔ" is a link: it opens the home position card again
  over the get-ready card, and closing that card (typed or skipped) comes back to the get-ready card. The space bar, Esc, a tap on the drawing or the round X (its tip says so) closes
  it and starts: the card is only a reminder, so every way out of it is the start.
- **The home position** ("Základná pozícia") is taught once, right after the new-key cards of
  F and J, before they are used in play: a card shows the home row of the child's layout (ASDF
  JKLÔ) as keys in their fingers' colors with the bumps on F and J. Its text, in short
  paragraphs: find the two keys with bumps for F and J; put the index fingers on them, close
  the eyes and play until they always find them again by touch alone, without looking; then
  the other fingers on A S D and K L Ô, the thumbs resting on the space bar; every exercise
  starts like this, the fingers come back here after each key, and while practising one never
  looks at the keyboard. The child confirms it by typing F and J by turns three times, a
  mark per key as on a new-key card (a space between them is fine). Esc skips it like a
  new-key card; no setting turns it off. Nowhere else does the game tell where the fingers go
  (not in the stories or the posture reminders), except the line under the picture of the
  get-ready card and the home position card it links to.
- **Before the very first new key** one card on the new-key card's model says, once: welcome,
  you learn to type with all ten fingers without looking; new keys come one by one, when the
  known ones are typed accurately enough or when another letter is due, and their card shows
  where the key is and which finger presses it; a key card closes once the key is typed right
  three times (typos do not count) or with Esc; and this card closes with the space bar, the
  longest key. The space bar, Enter, Esc, the drawn space bar or the round X closes it.
- **Control keys** steer the game but never count among the learned keys; each gets one card
  on the new-key card's model, once per profile, with its finger and short tips, closed by
  pressing the key (Esc skips it, except on its own card):
  - Esc, right after that first card: some keys do not count but matter; Esc stops the game,
    another Esc lets it go on, and it closes every card. On its own card Esc does not close it: each press is
    counted like a learned key, and the third one closes the card; the round X closes it at once.
  - Tab, in the level after the one where Enter first came: Tab moves between buttons and links
    on a page and Enter opens them (Shift + Tab goes back), so pages work without a mouse.
    Pressed three times.
  - Backspace, before the first crossroads, where it first matters: here it does not fix
    letters, it wipes the whole started choice (a sign, a group of words or a word) so the
    child can change their mind; in normal typing it deletes a letter and Ctrl + Backspace a
    word, but typing without mistakes matters more, since a fix costs at least three presses
    instead of one. Pressed three times.
- The level can be paused while its key cards are open too (the pause button): the pause card
  then has no "Slower", since nothing is played yet, and "Stop typing" goes back to the map.
  Keys whose cards were shown count as introduced only once play begins; leaving before that,
  the cards come again next time.
- A new key is introduced on its own card, compact enough to keep the keyboard in sight: the
  character large in a column of its own on the left, the key and "Nový kláves" in one heading
  line on the right, the finger's name highlighted in that finger's color. The finger line says
  to put the finger back on its resting key only when the key is not that one ("Stláčaj ho
  ľavým prostredníkom a potom vráť prst na D." for E, no return for D). Every key typed leaves a
  green check or a red cross after "Napíš d trikrát." (at most 10, the oldest make room) and jumps
  off the character's head in a random direction, fading after about a second and a half. The round X (its tip:
  skip the key practice, Esc) or Esc skips it. Texts say "základné miesta" for the home keys,
  never "domov"; game objects (the character's gloves) appear only when play starts. The space
  bar is pressed by a thumb; a Shift card names its hand and asks for three capitals of the
  other hand. For a capital the guide lights the Shift of the other hand. The keyboard has
  an Enter key at the end of the home row, pressed by the right little finger; the guide
  lights it when a target waits for its Enter
  ([lesson progression](lesson-progression.md)), with no card of its own.
- If the child holds the Shift on the same hand as the letter, the letter still counts and
  the narrator says once per level to use the other hand.
- Preparation tip: at the start of every training session one short, random tip about
  getting ready to type well (posture, hands, fingers, eyes, rhythm, breaks), shown on the
  get-ready card. A pool of 40+ tips per language, no repeat of the last few shown.

## Why
- Weak finger guidance is a frequent complaint (Dance Mat); masking or fading the keyboard
  is standard teacher advice
  ([research](../../.research/features/on-screen-keyboard-finger-guide.md)).
- Hands lying on the keys show where every finger rests and how far it reaches, which small
  hands beside the keyboard could not; a letter under a finger would be lost, so it is drawn over it.
- Finger numbers on every key were clutter beside the colors and the finger guide; a keyboard
  whose keys got flatter in a low window no longer looked like a keyboard. Rows of different key
  sizes put keys where no real keyboard has them (U right of J), so the fingers' reach shown on
  screen did not match the child's own keyboard.
- Enter comes rarely and is no lesson key, so the guide alone shows its finger; a new-key
  card for it would stop play for a key that does not open anything.
- Story scenes and a map greeting before the first level were many clicks and much text
  before the child typed anything; key cards and one get-ready card with the story are enough.
- Where the fingers go was told in the intro scene, the welcome, the posture tips and the
  card at once, and long before the keys were met; taught once with the first keys and
  confirmed by typing, it is learned instead of read past.
- Not looking at the keyboard is the habit that makes ten-finger typing; finding F and J by
  touch with closed eyes shows the child that the eyes are not needed, and "home position" is
  the name typing courses use. A child who forgot it finds the card again from the get-ready card.
- Several cards and the same story in a bubble right after the card were too many pop-ups
  before play; one card says it all, and the finger line comes only to a child who waits.
- Good habits (posture, hand position) are easier to build at the start than to fix
  later; short varied tips remind the child without a boring lecture.
- A list of five rules gets skipped; one reminder plus a tiny typing task gets read, and
  puts the fingers on the keys before the game begins.
